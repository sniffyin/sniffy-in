First Grooming for Puppies and Kittens in Pune

The first grooming visit decides how your pet feels about the dryer, the water and being handled for life. We keep it short, calm and on your pet’s own turf, after the vet says the time is right.

The short answer

Wait until your puppy or kitten has finished the vaccinations your vet advises, usually from around three to four months, before a first bath. At home it is a short, gentle ₹799 Spa Bath: warm water, plenty of breaks, and a quick introduction to the dryer and nail clipper. The aim is a good first experience, not a perfect coat.

  • Book after your vet confirms the vaccination course is complete
  • Spa Bath ₹799; a short, patient first visit, not a full groom
  • Dryer, paws and nails are introduced gradually, with breaks
  • Tick and flea products have minimum ages; check with your vet

Who a first groom is for

A first groom is less about getting clean and more about learning that grooming is safe. Most of the value is in how it is done.

New puppy owners

Your puppy needs a bath eventually, and the first one sets the tone. Doing it at home, with you present and no cage, is the gentlest way to start.

Kitten owners

Most kittens do not need a bath. A first visit may be a brush, a wipe and a nail trim, and we tell you honestly if a full bath can wait.

Rescued and adopted young animals

They arrive with unknown history, and sometimes with fleas. A careful first groom, after a vet check, helps them settle and spots problems early.

Breeds with fast-growing coats

Shih Tzu, Poodle and Maltese puppies grow coat quickly. Starting early with a tidy face and paws makes later trims much easier.

Puppies that mouth or wriggle

Totally normal. A puppy who nips at the dryer is learning. The groomer works with the energy rather than against it.

Families with children

A first groom at home means the children can watch from a distance, and nobody has to carry a nervous puppy through a lift or a salon waiting room.

What happens during a first grooming visit

The first visit is slower than a regular one on purpose. Each step is introduced, rewarded and only then repeated.

  1. 1. Meet and settle

    The groomer says hello, lets your pet sniff the equipment and sits on the floor for a few minutes. Treats are welcome; ask your vet if there are food limits.

  2. 2. Touch the paws, ears and tail

    Gentle handling of all the places that will be groomed later. Puppies that learn this young are far easier at every future visit.

  3. 3. Warm, short bath

    Lukewarm water, a mild puppy-appropriate shampoo and a hand-held rinse with no spray in the face. The bath is kept short, with the pet supported throughout.

  4. 4. Dry in stages

    The towel first, then the dryer on the lowest setting from a distance, so the sound and warmth arrive gradually.

  5. 5. A quick nail trim and ear check

    One or two nails if that is all your puppy will allow, rather than a battle over all four paws. Ears and eyes are checked and wiped.

  6. 6. A short note for you

    What went well, what your pet was unsure about, what to practise at home and when to book the next one.

Puppy & kitten first groom: what to know before you book

Every life stage

When can a puppy or kitten have its first bath?

Vaccinations first, then the groomer

There is no single age that fits every pet. The usual guidance is to wait until the vaccination course your vet has planned is complete, because a young animal’s protection is still building and a wet, chilled puppy is more vulnerable. For many puppies that is around three to four months.

Before that, a spot-clean with a damp cloth and a gentle wipe is usually enough, and you can introduce handling of paws, ears and the brush yourself. Ask your vet when the first full bath is safe for your pet, and mention it when you book.

For kittens the same rule applies, and the bar for a full bath is higher. Healthy kittens clean themselves well, so a first visit is often a brush, a wipe and a nail trim.

If in doubt, wait a week and ask the vet. A late first groom costs nothing; a rushed one can cost a lot.

At your doorstep

Why a first groom at home works better

No cage, no waiting room, no other animals

A salon is loud, new and full of other animals and smells. For a young pet it is a lot to take in while also being handled by a stranger. At home the surroundings are familiar, the floor smells of you and there is nobody barking in the next cage.

You can also stay. That matters more than most people expect. A puppy that can see you is far calmer than one that has been taken away, and you can learn how to hold, dry and brush your pet from someone showing you in your own bathroom.

And you save the journey. A car ride with a nervous puppy before the first bath is a stress you do not need to add.

  • Familiar surroundings and smells
  • You are there and your pet can see you
  • No other animals, noise or cages
  • You learn the technique for between-visit care

What we do

Making the first groom a good memory

Five small things that change the whole experience

Handle your puppy’s paws, ears and mouth gently for a few minutes a day in the week before the visit. Let them hear the hair dryer from another room. Try a clean towel and a brush on the floor so they are familiar objects, not strange ones.

Skip a big meal just before; a calm, empty-ish stomach is more comfortable in the bath. Have a few treats ready if your vet has no objection, and choose a time when your puppy is naturally sleepy.

During the visit, stay calm and relaxed. Pets read our voices and posture, and a puppy that senses anxiety in the room will mirror it.

  • Practise handling paws, ears and mouth beforehand
  • Let them hear the dryer from another room
  • A light meal, not a heavy one, before the visit
  • Treats and praise, as your vet allows
  • Stay calm: your pet takes cues from you

Vaccinations, worming and parasites around the first groom

What your vet decides and what we can add

The vaccination schedule is your vet’s call, not ours, and we ask you to tell us where your pet is in it. We will not groom a puppy that your vet has said should not be bathed yet.

Deworming and tick and flea products also depend on age and weight. Both are optional add-ons here, and only worth doing when your vet agrees. A spot-on that is safe for an adult dog can be unsafe for a young puppy or a kitten, which is why we ask.

If the groomer finds fleas, ticks, a smelly ear or a patch of broken skin, they will tell you and suggest a vet visit rather than treating it themselves.

We would rather skip an add-on than apply the wrong product to a very young animal.

What to do after the first groom

Build on a good start

Keep the momentum. Brush gently a few times a week, even for a minute, so the brush is a normal thing. Reward calm. A puppy that associates grooming with attention and treats will be easy to groom for the next decade.

Book the next visit within four to six weeks for most breeds, sooner for long coats. A quick bath, a tidy of the face and paws, and nails is plenty. By the third visit, most puppies hardly notice.

If your puppy hated it, say so. Tell the groomer what bothered them and we will slow that step down next time. Fear is much easier to fix when it is small.

Three good visits in a row are worth more than one perfect one.

Puppy & kitten first groom: frequently asked questions

At what age can a puppy have its first groom?

Usually after the vaccination course your vet has planned is complete, which is often around three to four months. Ask your vet for the date. Before then, spot-clean with a damp cloth and practise handling paws, ears and the brush at home.

When can a kitten be groomed?

Healthy kittens clean themselves and rarely need a bath early on. Wait for your vet’s all-clear on vaccinations. A first visit is usually a brush, a wipe and a nail trim, and the groomer will say honestly if a bath can wait.

How much does a puppy’s first groom cost in Pune?

The Spa Bath is ₹799, the same as for any dog. There is no separate puppy price and no size surcharge. Optional add-ons such as Deworming (₹299) or Tick & Flea Care (₹149) are only worth adding if your vet agrees.

Is a haircut safe for a first groom?

It can be, but we usually suggest a bath first so your puppy gets used to the noise and handling. Fast-growing breeds like Shih Tzus and Poodles can have a face and paw tidy early, with a full haircut at the second or third visit.

My puppy is scared of the dryer. What do we do?

The groomer starts with a towel and lets the dryer run on low from a distance, getting closer only as your puppy relaxes. It is a gradual introduction, not an ambush. If your puppy cannot cope, we stop and allow more time at the next visit.

Can I be there during the first groom?

Yes, and we encourage it. Your puppy is calmer when they can see you, and you will learn the right technique for brushing, drying and handling between visits.

Do you sedate puppies or kittens?

Never. We use patience, breaks and gentle handling. If a young pet is too distressed to continue, we stop, explain what we achieved and schedule a shorter follow-up visit.

Is tick and flea treatment safe for puppies and kittens?

Only when the product is licensed for their age and weight, which your vet can confirm. Spot-on treatments have minimum ages, and some dog products are dangerous for cats. We apply the add-on only when you have confirmed it is suitable.

How long does a puppy’s first groom take?

Usually about an hour for a small puppy, longer for a large breed or a nervous one. We allow extra time and build in breaks because the aim is a calm experience, not a fast one.

How often should a puppy be groomed after the first time?

Every four to six weeks is a good rhythm for most breeds, a bit more often for long or curly coats. Short, regular visits teach a puppy that grooming is routine, which makes every later visit easier.
