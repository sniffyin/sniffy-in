Who a de-shedding bath actually helps

Shedding is not the same everywhere. Breeds with two layers of coat hold on to dead hair until something pulls it out, and this treatment is that something.

Huskies and Malamutes

Two full coat blows a year, each one lasting weeks. Pune’s heat makes owners want to shave; the groomer removes the dead layer instead, which is what actually cools the dog.

Labradors and Golden Retrievers

They shed lightly all year and heavily twice a year. A de-shed every month keeps the flat tolerable and stops the coat from compacting into felted patches behind the ears and tail.

German Shepherds and Pomeranians

Thick, dense undercoats that trap hair against the skin. Left alone, the trapped coat holds moisture through the monsoon and the skin underneath sours.

Beagles, Pugs and other short coats

Short does not mean low shedding. The hairs are fine and sharp, they work into upholstery and a bath with a good rubber brush clears them quickly.

Indie dogs

Many Indies carry a seasonal undercoat after the monsoon. The same treatment applies, and it is usually the quickest job of the lot.

Cats, especially Persians and Maine Coons

Cats swallow what they shed, and that comes back as hairballs. Removing loose coat in the bath means less of it ends up in the stomach.

How the de-shedding treatment works

Same bathroom, same groomer from start to finish. Here is what happens in the order it happens, and why the order matters.

  1. 1. Read the coat before touching it

    The groomer checks how much dead undercoat there is and whether any of it has packed into mats. Mats are dealt with first, because shampoo tightens them.

  2. 2. A dry pre-brush

    A slicker and an undercoat rake go through the dry coat in sections. This clears the loose top layer so the shampoo reaches skin, not a wall of hair.

  3. 3. Bath with a conditioning shampoo

    Lukewarm water, a coat-appropriate shampoo and a conditioner worked in with the hands. Conditioner is what loosens the dead hair’s grip, so it is never skipped.

  4. 4. Rinse until the water runs clear

    Leftover conditioner is the most common cause of a coat that looks dull and itches. The groomer rinses longer than feels necessary.

  5. 5. The long blow-out

    This is the heart of the treatment. A dryer pushes the loosened undercoat out while the groomer brushes in the direction of the hair. It takes the most time and gives the most result.

  6. 6. Final brush and a tidy-up

    A last brush-through, ears, paws and tail checked for stray mats, and the bathroom left as it was found, minus the fur, which goes into a bag.

De-shedding: what to know before you book

Pune seasons

Why Pune dogs seem to shed all year

Weather that never quite commits to a season

Dogs shed in response to daylight and temperature. Pune has neither a real winter nor a clear spring, so the cycle blurs. A Labrador that would drop a coat twice a year in a colder city here sheds a bit every week, plus two heavier bursts, usually after the monsoon and before the peak of summer.

That is why owners say “he never stops”. He does stop, briefly, and then the next burst begins. A monthly de-shedding bath flattens those bursts so there is never a week where the coat all comes out at once.

Flats make it worse. Hair has nowhere to go but the fans, the sofa and the air conditioner filter, and a ceiling fan carries it into every room.

  • Heavy bursts are usually after the monsoon and before peak summer
  • A light, steady shed in between is normal for Pune
  • Regular baths stop the burst from arriving all at once

Less hair on the floor is the visible result; a coat that can breathe is the one that matters.

Coat & skin care

Never shave a double coat to stop shedding

The most common mistake we are asked to make

Shaving looks like the fix. It is not. A double coat is two layers doing two jobs: the undercoat insulates, the guard hairs reflect sun and shed water. Shave them and the dog is more exposed to heat and sunburn, not less, and the coat that grows back can come in patchy and coarse.

It also does not stop shedding. A shaved Husky still sheds; the hairs are just shorter and sharper, and they weave into upholstery instead of floating off it.

So we say no, politely, and offer the de-shed. If you want a shorter, easier look in summer, a light trim to the length of a hand is the most we do, and only on coats that can take it.

Coat & skin care

What a de-shedding treatment will and will not do

Honest expectations, so you are not disappointed

It will remove a large part of the dead coat that was about to drop. From a Golden or a Shepherd the groomer can often fill a carrier bag, and that is hair that does not end up in your home this fortnight.

It will not stop shedding, change how much your dog sheds in total or fix a diet or skin problem. If your dog is shedding in clumps, scratching, or has patchy or thin areas, that is not a coat issue. See a vet before any grooming treatment, because bathing over a skin infection can make it worse.

What you get from a regular schedule is predictability: smaller bursts, a cleaner flat and a coat that stays smooth rather than felting.

  • Removes the dead undercoat that is ready to drop
  • Does not change how much hair your dog makes
  • Does not treat skin disease, allergies or poor nutrition
  • Bald patches, scabs or constant itching: vet first

Grooming frequency

How often to book a de-shed

A schedule that matches the coat, not a calendar

For heavy shedders such as Huskies, Shepherds and Goldens, every three to four weeks keeps the coat under control. During a full coat blow you can book as often as every two weeks and see a real difference each time, because there is that much loose hair to remove.

Labradors and other medium shedders do well on a four to six week rhythm. Short coats and Indies are fine on a bath every six to eight weeks with de-shedding added when they are shedding noticeably.

Between visits, a few minutes of brushing every other day with a slicker and a comb extends the result and costs nothing. The groomer shows you the right tool for your dog’s coat at the visit.

If you can see hair on the floor within a day of the bath, the schedule is too long for the coat.

Coat & skin care

De-shedding for cats

Less hair swallowed, fewer hairballs

Cats are different. They tolerate far less handling than dogs, so the cat version is shorter and quieter: a gentle brush, a bath only if the cat needs one and a calm blow-out, in one closed room so nobody escapes.

Persians and long-haired cats get the most from it, because loose hair left in the coat mats against the skin. Short-haired domestic cats can shed heavily in summer, and removing the loose coat means less of it ends up as hairballs.

If a cat will not tolerate the dryer, we stop. We do not sedate cats and we do not force a bath. The groomer will brush what the cat allows and tell you what was possible.

A cat that tolerates ten calm minutes is a better outcome than a cat groomed against its will.

De-shedding: frequently asked questions

How much does de-shedding cost in Pune with Sniffy?

It is a ₹299 add-on on top of the Spa Bath, which is ₹799, so ₹1,098 in total. The price is the same for every breed and size, and there is no doorstep charge anywhere in Pune.

Will de-shedding stop my dog from shedding?

No, and any service that promises that is misleading you. It removes the dead undercoat that was about to fall out, which cuts shedding noticeably for a few weeks. Your dog keeps growing new hair, so the cycle continues and the treatment is repeated.

How often should I get my dog de-shedded?

Every three to four weeks for heavy shedders such as Huskies, Shepherds and Goldens, every four to six weeks for Labradors and medium shedders, and only when needed for short-coated dogs. During a full coat blow you can book every two weeks.

Which breeds benefit most from de-shedding?

Double-coated breeds: Siberian Huskies, Alaskan Malamutes, Golden Retrievers, Labradors, German Shepherds, Pomeranians, Chow Chows and Saint Bernards. Cats such as Persians and Maine Coons benefit too. Single-coated dogs that shed lightly, like Poodles, rarely need it.

Is it safe to shave my Husky or Golden Retriever instead?

Please do not. The undercoat insulates against heat as well as cold, and the guard hairs shield the skin from sun. A shaved double coat can overheat, sunburn and grow back patchy. We decline to shave double coats and offer the de-shed, which is what actually helps.

How long does a de-shedding session take?

Usually one and a half to two hours for a large double-coated dog, and an hour to an hour and a half for a medium one. The long blow-out is where most of the time goes, and rushing it defeats the purpose.

My dog is shedding in clumps and scratching. Is that normal?

It might not be. Normal shedding is even and the skin underneath looks healthy. Clumps, bald or thin patches, redness, scabs, a smell or constant scratching point to allergies, parasites, a fungal infection or a nutritional problem. See your vet first; a bath over an untreated skin problem can make it worse.

Can I get de-shedding for my cat?

Yes. The cat version is shorter and calmer: brushing, a bath only if needed and a gentle blow-out in a closed room. It is especially useful for Persians and heavy summer shedders. We never sedate a cat, and we stop if the cat is too stressed.

Does de-shedding include a haircut?

No. It is a bath-based treatment. Double-coated dogs should not be shaved, so if you want a trim for another reason, such as a Poodle or a Shih Tzu, book the Spa Bath + Hair Cut package at ₹1,299 and add de-shedding if the coat needs it.

What do I need to arrange before the groomer arrives?

A bathroom or a washable corner with a tap, a plug point for the dryer and enough floor space. Brush your dog lightly beforehand if you can, mention any mats or skin concerns when you book and keep the doors closed so the loose hair does not travel.
