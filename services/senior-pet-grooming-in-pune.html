When a pet counts as senior, and what changes

Large breeds age faster than small ones, so “senior” starts at about seven for a Great Dane and about eleven for a Pomeranian. What matters is how your pet moves and copes.

Dogs with stiff hips or arthritis

Standing on a wet floor is hard work. A mat, a supporting hand under the hindquarters and shorter standing time make the bath comfortable rather than tiring.

Dogs with weak back legs

Some older dogs cannot stand long at all. The groomer works with the dog lying or sitting where possible, and splits the job into shorter parts.

Older cats that groom themselves less

Stiffness and dental pain make cats stop reaching their back and belly. Mats and a greasy coat build up there, and nails grow long because they scratch less.

Pets with thinning or sensitive skin

Older skin tears and dries more easily. The groomer uses a mild shampoo, lukewarm water and a soft hand with the brush.

Pets with hearing or sight loss

They can be startled easily. The groomer approaches from the front, speaks softly, touches before moving and avoids sudden dryer noise near the head.

Pets on long-term medication

Tell us at booking. We ask what the medication is for, avoid anything that could clash and never change a vet’s plan.

How we change a grooming visit for an older pet

Nothing about the quality of the clean changes. The steps are the same; the pace, support and expectations are different.

  1. 1. Ask about health first

    Before anything starts, the groomer asks about mobility, medication, recent surgery, lumps and any places that hurt to touch. These shape the plan for the whole visit.

  2. 2. Set up the bathroom safely

    A non-slip mat down, towels ready within reach, the water tested on the wrist. Slips cause far more harm to an older pet than a slightly longer bath.

  3. 3. Warm, short, supported bath

    Lukewarm water, a mild shampoo and one hand always supporting the chest or hindquarters. Rest breaks whenever the pet shifts, pants or lies down.

  4. 4. Dry gently, and fully

    Towel first, then the dryer on a low, warm setting from a distance. Older pets chill easily and damp coats can cause skin problems, so the drying is thorough.

  5. 5. Nails, ears and a light touch of tidy

    Nails are clipped gently, ears and eyes cleaned, and any stray mats dealt with without wrestling. The hair cut, if wanted, is shorter and neater rather than fussy.

  6. 6. Notes for you and the vet

    Lumps, sore spots, weight changes, dental issues or skin changes are written down and shared with you. We do not diagnose; we notice and report.

Senior pet grooming: what to know before you book

What we do

Why older pets need a different bath, not a different groomer

Comfort is the whole point

A young dog tolerates twenty minutes on a wet floor. An older dog with sore hips may not manage five. The bath itself is the same, but the setup has to fit the body: a rubber mat so paws do not slide, a hand under the hindquarters, water the temperature of a warm bath, and no pressure to finish quickly.

The same applies to cats. An old cat is rarely a willing participant, but a quiet room, a towel to hold on to and a groomer who respects when it has had enough can turn a dreaded event into a tolerable one.

A short, calm visit is a good visit. If your pet can only manage part of it, we do that part, and leave the rest for another day.

Fewer minutes and more comfort beats a perfect coat every time.

What we do

Lumps, bumps and what a groomer notices

The advantage of a hands-on visit

During a bath a groomer’s hands pass over every inch of the body. In an older pet that often means finding something new: a lump under the skin, a patch of thinning hair, a sore spot that makes the pet flinch, or a smell from the ears or mouth.

We cannot diagnose and we do not try to. What we do is tell you clearly what we found, where and when it seemed new to us, so you can show your vet. Most lumps are harmless; some are not, and a note made early can matter.

A weekly head-to-tail check at home is the easiest way to track change in an older pet, and the guide below shows you how.

  • New lumps or swellings anywhere on the body
  • Weight loss or a pet that feels thinner under the coat
  • Sore spots, limping or flinching on touch
  • Smelly ears, bad breath or red, dry or flaky skin

Pune seasons

Pune’s heat, monsoon and older pets

Seasons that are harder at fourteen than at four

Older pets regulate heat less well. In a Pune summer we book early or late in the day, keep the room cool and avoid long dryer sessions in a hot bathroom. Panting, drooling and lethargy mean stop, cool the pet and let it recover.

In the monsoon, damp is the enemy. Older skin is thinner and slower to recover, and a coat that stays wet under the surface becomes an infection waiting to happen. Thorough, gentle drying is not optional, and we do not leave until the coat is dry to the skin.

Winter mornings in Pune are cooler than they look; wrap an older pet after the bath and keep it out of drafts for an hour or so.

Your pet cannot tell you it is too hot or too cold. We watch for the signs.

Special Handling: when an older dog needs more hands

An optional extra at +₹199

Some older dogs are heavy, some are frail, and some are simply no longer sure about being handled. Special Handling adds extra time, patience and sometimes a second pair of hands to support a dog that cannot easily stand or settle on its own.

It is also useful for dogs who have developed anxiety with age, perhaps from poor hearing or vision or from pain they have learned to expect. The groomer works at the dog’s speed and stops when the dog says so.

It is optional and we only suggest it when we believe it will genuinely help. Many gentle seniors do perfectly well on a standard bath.

If you are not sure, mention it at booking and the groomer will advise.

Senior cats: the small things matter most

Quiet room, slow hands, short visit

Older cats get mats where they can no longer reach, nails that curl into the pads, and a coat that turns greasy near the tail. They may also be thinner, with skin that tears easily and a lower tolerance for stress.

The groomer works in one closed room with a warm towel and no rush. A full bath is not always needed; a brush, a spot-clean, a nail trim and a mat removal may be kinder. The aim is comfort, not perfection.

We never sedate a cat. If your senior cat has a heart or kidney condition, tell us at booking and ask your vet whether grooming stress is a concern.

A short visit that leaves your cat calm is better than a long one that does not.

Senior pet grooming: frequently asked questions

At what age is a dog or cat considered senior?

It depends on size. Large and giant dogs are senior from around six to eight years, medium dogs from eight to ten, small dogs and cats from about ten to eleven. What matters more than age is how your pet moves, copes and recovers.

How much does senior pet grooming cost in Pune?

There is no senior surcharge. A Spa Bath is ₹799 and a Hair Cut ₹999. Dogs who need extra support can add Special Handling for +₹199. Cats get the same slower pace as part of the standard visit.

Can an arthritic dog be bathed safely?

Yes, with changes: a non-slip mat, warm water, a hand supporting the hindquarters, shorter standing time and rests. If your dog is in pain or limping badly, speak to your vet first; some dogs benefit from a plan for pain control before grooming.

My old dog gets tired quickly. Can the visit be split?

Yes. The groomer does the essentials first, usually the bath, drying and nails, and leaves the trim for another day if your dog tires. We would rather finish early than push an older pet past its limit.

What if my pet has a heart condition or is on medication?

Tell us at booking. We check with you what is safe, keep the visit calm and cool, and stop if the pet shows distress. For serious conditions, ask your vet whether grooming is advisable and mention anything they advise.

Will you tell me if you find a lump?

Yes. We note any new lumps, sore areas, skin changes or smells and tell you at the end of the visit. We never diagnose, but a clear note about where and when you noticed something helps your vet.

Is it safe to groom an old cat?

Usually yes, if kept short and calm. We use one closed room, no sedation and stop if the cat is stressed. If your cat has heart, kidney or breathing problems, ask your vet first.

My dog is incontinent or has accidents. Is that a problem?

Not at all. It is common in older dogs and we handle it matter-of-factly. Mention it when you book so the groomer comes prepared, and we focus on keeping your dog clean and comfortable.

Should I choose a haircut for my older dog?

A short, easy-care cut often helps older dogs whose coat has become hard to brush or mats easily. The groomer can do it in stages. A shorter coat is also easier to keep clean if your dog has accidents.

Do I need to be home during the session?

Please, yes, or have someone your pet knows well nearby. Familiar company keeps an older pet calmer, and you can tell the groomer straight away if anything seems wrong.
