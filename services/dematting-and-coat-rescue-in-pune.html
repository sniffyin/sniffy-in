The short answer

De-matting is the careful removal of tangles and mats from a dog or cat’s coat. At Sniffy it is a ₹299 add-on to the ₹799 Spa Bath. Light and moderate mats are combed out; mats that are tight against the skin are clipped, because pulling them out hurts. The groomer decides which, and tells you before starting.

  • ₹299 add-on to the ₹799 Spa Bath, for dogs and cats
  • Combs out what can be saved without pain; clips what cannot
  • Tightly matted coats usually need the ₹999 Hair Cut instead
  • Matting to the skin with sores or a smell: see a vet as well

Who needs de-matting, and why it happens

Mats are not neglect. They are what happens when loose hair, moisture and friction meet a coat that cannot shed the loose hair on its own.

Shih Tzus, Lhasa Apsos and Maltese

Long, fine, silky hair that grows continuously. Daily brushing keeps it clear; a missed fortnight in humid weather is often enough to start a mat behind the ears.

Poodles and Doodles

Curly coats that hold shed hair instead of releasing it. The hair knots against itself from the skin outward, which is why mats feel solid.

Persian and other long-haired cats

Cats tolerate far less brushing than dogs, so mats form in the armpits, belly and behind the ears. Their skin is thin and tears easily, which makes careful work important.

Golden Retrievers and Cocker Spaniels

Feathering on the ears, legs and tail rubs and tangles. The mats start small and spread, and are usually hidden under the longer outer hair.

Dogs recovering from a long gap between groomings

A coat left for several months after the monsoon. It is common, it is fixable, and nobody on the visit will judge it.

Senior pets who groom less

Stiff joints and dental pain mean older cats and dogs stop reaching the places they used to. Their coat tells you before they do.

How the groomer rescues a matted coat

The order matters. Water makes mats tighter, so the work starts dry, and the decision about what can be saved is made before anything is pulled.

  1. 1. Assess every mat on the dry coat

    The groomer runs hands over the whole body and finds the mats, including those hiding at the skin. They sort mats into loose, moderate and tight.

  2. 2. Tell you what will happen

    Before any tool touches the coat, you hear the plan: which areas will be combed out, which will be clipped short and why. Nothing is a surprise at the end.

  3. 3. Loosen the saveable mats

    A conditioning spray and a wide comb, working from the outside of each mat inward in small steps. The goal is to split a mat gently, not to rip it out.

  4. 4. Clip what cannot be saved

    Tight or pelted mats are clipped close to the coat with the skin protected by a comb underneath. This is faster, kinder and safer than brushing through a mat that is pulling the skin.

  5. 5. Bath and condition

    Once the mats are out, the bath and conditioner go on, so the freshly freed coat is clean and smooth rather than dragged back into knots.

  6. 6. Dry, brush and a care plan

    A thorough dry and brush, then a short, specific plan: which tool, how often and where the mats formed so you know where to look next time.

De-matting: what to know before you book

Coat & skin care

Why a trim is sometimes kinder than combing

The decision most owners are afraid to hear

A mat is not just tangled hair. Close to the skin it tightens as the coat moves, pulling on the skin with every step. Combing a tight mat means pulling that skin further with every stroke. For the dog or cat it is the same as having a clump of hair yanked repeatedly.

So the rule is simple. If a mat can be loosened with a spray and a wide comb in a few gentle strokes, we do that. If it needs force, we clip it. A short coat that is comfortable is a better outcome than a long coat that is a battle.

Hair grows back. A dog or cat that has learned that grooming is painful does not forget.

You will always be told the plan first, and if you want to try combing a particular spot, the groomer will try.

Pune seasons

Pune’s humidity and the mats that follow

Why June to September is mat season

Damp coat is the fastest route to a mat. During the monsoon a walk, a puddle or a wet paw leaves the coat moist for hours, and hair clumps together as it dries. Add loose coat that has nowhere to go and mats appear in days.

The places to check are where friction meets moisture: behind the ears, under the collar or harness, the armpits, the inside of the back legs, the belly and the base of the tail. A harness that is never taken off in the monsoon is a quiet mat factory.

A five-minute check with a metal comb, reaching down to the skin rather than gliding over the top, finds a mat while it is still small enough to tease out at home.

  • Behind the ears and under the collar or harness
  • Armpits and the inside of the back legs
  • Belly and the base of the tail
  • Persian cats: around the neck ruff and the hind end

Coat & skin care

De-matting a cat

Thin skin, strong opinions

Cat skin is much thinner and looser than dog skin. A cat that twists during a clip can be nicked easily, which is why mats on cats are never rushed and never cut with scissors against the skin.

The groomer works in one closed room and watches body language: flattened ears, a swishing tail and growling mean a break. We do not sedate cats. If a cat cannot tolerate the work, we stop, tell you how much was possible and agree what to do next, possibly a vet visit for the rest.

For a Persian that has gone badly matted, the lion cut is often the kinder reset. It is a choice, not a failure, and the blog post below explains when it makes sense.

The hard part for most owners is deciding to act. Waiting makes the mats larger and the decision harder.

Coat & skin care

Keeping the coat mat-free between visits

What actually works, and what does not

A pin brush glides across the top and leaves the undercoat alone, which is how people brush daily and still find mats. Use a wide-toothed metal comb, going down to the skin in sections, and finish with a brush. If the comb snags, go slower on that spot rather than pulling.

For long-coated breeds, brush every day or every other day. For curly coats, a thorough comb at least twice a week. A leave-in conditioning spray makes the comb glide and costs little.

Short, regular trims are the other half. Breeds like Shih Tzus and Poodles are far easier to keep comfortable in a short, tidy cut that is repeated every six to eight weeks than in a long coat nobody has time to brush.

  • A metal comb that reaches the skin, not just a brush
  • Daily for long coats, twice weekly for curly coats
  • A conditioning spray to help the comb glide
  • A short cut repeated every 6 to 8 weeks

De-matting: frequently asked questions

How much does de-matting cost in Pune with Sniffy?

De-matting is a ₹299 add-on to the ₹799 Spa Bath, ₹1,098 in total. If the coat is too heavily matted to comb out, a full-body trim on the Hair Cut package at ₹999 is often the kinder and faster choice. The groomer tells you which before starting.

Is it painful for my dog or cat?

It should not be. We work on the dry coat, use a conditioning spray and a wide comb, and stop pulling the moment it hurts. Where a mat is tight to the skin we clip it instead, because combing it would hurt. A matted coat is already uncomfortable, and the relief afterwards is usually obvious.

Why can’t you just brush the mats out?

A mat tightens as the coat moves and is often attached to the skin. Brushing through it pulls the skin with every stroke. Light mats come out with a spray and a comb. Dense, tight mats, called pelting, need to be clipped, as anything else is cruel.

Will you shave my dog completely?

Only if that is what the coat needs. The groomer clips the affected areas first and shortens the rest to a length that looks even and is easy to maintain. On double-coated breeds we do not shave. You are told the plan before the clippers come out.

My dog has not been groomed for months. Is it too late?

No. Coats left for a season are common and fixable, usually with a full trim. We do not judge, we just ask you to say so when you book so the groomer allows enough time.

Can you de-mat a cat?

Yes, carefully. Cat skin is thin, so mats are never cut tight to the skin with scissors. We work in a closed room, watch for stress signs and never sedate. If the cat cannot tolerate it, we stop and advise a vet for the rest.

How long does it take?

A lightly matted dog takes about an hour and a half in total. A heavily matted one can run to two and a half or three hours including the bath, with breaks. The groomer tells you at the start if the coat will take longer.

Which breeds mat the most in Pune?

Shih Tzus, Lhasa Apsos, Maltese, Poodles, Doodles, Cocker Spaniels, Golden Retrievers with heavy feathering and Persian cats. Humid months, harnesses and long gaps between grooms make it worse.

What if there are sores or a bad smell under the mats?

Tell the groomer. Mats can trap moisture against the skin and cause irritation or infection. We will clip carefully, avoid the sore areas and recommend your vet see the skin. We do not treat skin infections ourselves.

How do I stop my dog from matting again?

Comb down to the skin with a metal comb every day or two for long coats, use a conditioning spray, check behind the ears and under the harness, and keep the coat shorter. A tidy cut every six to eight weeks is usually the single biggest help.
