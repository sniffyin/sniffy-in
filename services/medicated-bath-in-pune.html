Who a medicated bath is for

It is a treatment step, not a luxury. It is used when the skin is the problem and a plain shampoo is not enough.

Dogs with ringworm or yeast infections

Fungal skin disease is common in Pune’s humid months. Your vet confirms it and often recommends regular anti-fungal baths alongside any other treatment.

Dogs with bacterial skin infections

Red, spotty or pustular skin, often after scratching. A medicated shampoo helps reduce the bacteria on the surface while the vet treats the underlying cause.

Pets with chronic skin conditions

Allergies, seborrhoea and recurring hot spots can need maintenance baths on a vet’s schedule to keep the skin under control.

Cats with skin or fungal trouble

Cats can carry ringworm, which also passes to people. Medicated baths for cats are done gently, only when advised and in a closed room.

Pets after a flea or tick infestation

Heavy scratching can leave raw or infected skin. The vet may suggest a medicated bath once the parasites are controlled.

Dogs with smelly, greasy coats

Persistent odour with greasy, flaky skin is a skin issue, not a dirt issue. A vet visit comes first; a medicated bath may follow.

How a medicated bath is done properly

The difference between a good and a pointless medicated bath is almost entirely contact time and rinsing. Here is how it runs.

  1. 1. Confirm the vet’s advice

    The groomer asks what your vet diagnosed and recommended, and uses the product they suggest if you have one. If there is no vet advice, we recommend seeing one first.

  2. 2. Wet the coat thoroughly

    The entire coat is wetted to the skin with lukewarm water. A medicated shampoo applied to a half-wet coat does not reach the skin.

  3. 3. Apply the shampoo and massage in

    The shampoo is worked down to the skin everywhere, including skin folds, the belly, between the toes and under the tail, while avoiding the eyes and ears.

  4. 4. Leave it on for the full contact time

    Most medicated shampoos need to sit on the skin for several minutes, often around ten, to work. The groomer sets a timer and keeps your pet comfortable and warm.

  5. 5. Rinse until nothing is left

    Residue irritates skin. The groomer rinses far longer than for a normal bath, until the water runs completely clear.

  6. 6. Dry thoroughly, and skip the heat

    Damp skin feeds fungus. The coat is dried completely to the skin, with a warm, not hot, dryer, and your pet is kept away from damp bedding.

Medicated bath: what to know before you book

Pune seasons

Skin problems in Pune: why the monsoon makes it worse

Humid months and the skin they favour

Pune’s monsoon traps moisture in the coat for hours after a walk, and between the toes, under the belly and in skin folds it can stay damp all day. Fungus and bacteria thrive in warm damp skin, and the result shows up as itching, redness, hair loss, a musty smell or rings of flaky skin.

Pets with thick coats, floppy ears and skin folds are especially prone: Labradors, Pugs, Bulldogs, Cocker Spaniels and Shih Tzus. Dogs that swim or get wet often also suffer more.

A vet visit is always the right first step. A medicated bath works best as part of a plan the vet has made, not as a guess.

  • Itching, redness or a rash
  • Hair loss in circular or patchy areas
  • A musty smell or greasy, flaky skin
  • Dark, thickened skin in folds

The earlier it is treated, the shorter the treatment and the smaller the patch.

Services explained

Why contact time is everything

The step that decides if it works

Medicated shampoos contain active ingredients such as chlorhexidine or antifungals that need time on the skin to work. A normal bath lasts a few minutes; a medicated bath needs the shampoo to sit on the skin for its full label time, which is often around ten minutes.

That is why a medicated bath takes longer, and why one rushed at the end of a normal bath does very little. The groomer sets a timer, keeps your pet warm and comfortable, and does not rinse early.

The same applies to the rinse. Leftover shampoo irritates skin, especially skin that is already inflamed. We rinse longer than feels needed.

A medicated bath done in a hurry is a normal bath with a higher price. Ours takes the time it needs.

What we do

What a medicated bath cannot do

Being honest about its limits

It cannot diagnose. A groomer sees skin, not the cause. Red skin can be allergy, fungus, bacteria, mites, a hormonal problem or a reaction to food, and the treatments are different. Only a vet can sort them out, sometimes with a skin scrape or test.

It cannot replace prescribed medicine. Many fungal and bacterial infections need tablets, ointments or a longer course. The bath supports that treatment; it is not the treatment.

And it cannot fix a problem that is going on at home. Damp bedding, an untreated flea allergy or the wrong diet will bring the skin problem back however many baths you give.

  • It does not diagnose the cause of a skin problem
  • It does not replace prescribed medication
  • It does not cure fleas, allergies or diet issues
  • Skin with open sores or heavy infection: vet before groomer

Cat grooming

Medicated baths for cats

Handle with extra care

Ringworm and skin infections are less common in cats but harder to treat, and ringworm can pass to humans. The vet decides the plan; the bath is a supporting step and only if advised.

Cats do not always tolerate long contact times in water. The groomer works in a closed room, with warm water and a calm pace, and stops if the cat becomes distressed. We never sedate a cat for a bath.

If someone in the household has an itchy circular rash while a cat has skin trouble, mention it to your doctor and your vet.

With cats, a short, calm bath done carefully is better than a thorough one done under stress.

Grooming frequency

How often should a medicated bath be done?

Your vet sets the schedule

It depends on the condition and the product. Some courses ask for a bath once or twice a week for several weeks; maintenance for a long-term condition may be every two to four weeks. We follow the schedule your vet gives.

Too frequent bathing can dry the skin, and too little lets the problem return. Do not extend or shorten a course because the skin looks better; many infections look fine long before they are gone.

Between baths, keep the coat dry, change bedding often and avoid long wet walks if your pet’s skin is vulnerable.

When in doubt, ask your vet how many baths and for how long, and tell the groomer.

Medicated bath: frequently asked questions

How much does a medicated bath cost in Pune with Sniffy?

A Medicated Bath is a ₹299 add-on to the ₹799 Spa Bath, ₹1,098 in total, for dogs and cats. There is no size surcharge or doorstep charge.

Do I need a vet’s advice first?

Yes, we strongly recommend it. A medicated bath is a treatment step for a diagnosed skin condition. Skin problems have many causes and the wrong treatment can make things worse. Please see a vet first if you have not.

Can I use the shampoo my vet prescribed?

Yes. Keep it ready and tell us when you book. The groomer uses it as the label and your vet’s directions say, including the contact time.

How long does a medicated bath take?

Roughly an hour and a half to two hours, because the shampoo has to stay on the skin for its full contact time and the rinse and drying are thorough. Large or thick-coated dogs take longer.

Will a medicated bath cure my dog’s skin infection?

It supports treatment but rarely cures on its own. Many infections need medication from your vet as well. Keep following your vet’s plan, and book follow-up visits if they advise.

How often should my dog have a medicated bath?

As often as your vet tells you. Some courses are once or twice a week for a few weeks; maintenance may be every two to four weeks. We follow your vet’s schedule.

Is it safe for cats?

It can be, with the right product and a vet’s advice. Cats are bathed in a closed room, kept warm, and we stop if the cat is too stressed. We never sedate a cat.

My dog smells bad. Do I need a medicated bath?

Not necessarily. A bad smell can be dirt, ears, teeth, anal glands or a skin infection. Have a vet look first. If the cause is skin, a medicated bath may follow.

Can ringworm spread to me or my children?

Yes, ringworm is a fungal infection that can pass from pets to people. Wash your hands after handling your pet, avoid sharing bedding and see a doctor if you notice a circular itchy rash. Tell the groomer if your pet has ringworm.

What should I do after the bath?

Keep your pet in a clean, dry place, change bedding and follow your vet’s advice on medication. Wash bedding regularly, avoid damp areas and watch for skin changes. If things get worse, go back to your vet.
