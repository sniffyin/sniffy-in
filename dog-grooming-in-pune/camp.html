Frequently asked questions

Do you groom in bungalow properties in Camp?

Yes. Bungalow gardens and covered verandas are excellent grooming spaces. We work outdoors where the space allows and bring everything needed including water containers if access is a concern.

Do you have experience with Boxers and their skin fold care?

Yes. Boxer skin fold cleaning is a specific skill that's part of every session for this breed. We work through each fold carefully to prevent moisture buildup and irritation.

How do you handle elderly dogs from long-standing family homes?

Senior dogs in established families are among our most carefully handled clients. We slow the session, provide breaks, and approach the whole process with the patience that a long-standing family companion deserves.

Is cat grooming available in Camp?

Yes. We groom cats including Persians and domestic long-haired breeds across Camp.

What notice do you need for a booking in Camp?

24–48 hours is ideal. We can sometimes accommodate shorter notice. Contact us via WhatsApp for the fastest response.

Are your groomers discreet about operating within residential properties?

Yes. We operate with professionalism and quiet efficiency. We never cause disruption to neighbouring properties and leave no visible trace of the session outside the immediate working area.

Does the cantonment board require anything for a service visitor to enter?

Your gate's normal process, nothing more. The groomer carries photo identification, and we can send you their name and number ahead of the slot so entry can be logged in advance. Cantonment properties tend to be stricter about pre-notification than civilian societies, so it is worth arranging before the visit rather than at the barrier.

Parking around East Street and MG Road is impossible. Is that a problem?

Not for us. Groomers arrive by two-wheeler with the entire kit in one shoulder bag — no van, no bay, nothing unloaded at your gate. It is precisely why doorstep grooming is practical in the older, tighter parts of Camp at all.

We live in an old cantonment bungalow. Is the water and power setup an issue?

Rarely. The groomer needs a tap for roughly twenty minutes and one working plug point for the dryer. Old bungalows sometimes have both a long way from where the dog is comfortable, so if the nearest usable socket is in another room, mention it — the groomer will bring the working area to the plug rather than run a dog around the house.

Do you handle less common breeds that older Camp families tend to keep?

Yes — Boxers, Dobermans, Great Danes, Cocker Spaniels and Dachshunds are all routine here, and each needs something specific: fold cleaning on a Boxer, ear care on a Cocker, careful back handling on a Dachshund. If you have a breed we would not see often, tell us at booking so we send a groomer who has actually worked that coat.

We are relocating abroad with our dog. What grooming should happen first?

A full bath two to three days before the flight so the coat is completely dry before crate time, nails taken short, and paw and sanitary hair trimmed. Those affect crate comfort far more than a fresh haircut does. Schedule tightly around the vet health certificate, not around the grooming — a bath on travel morning leaves a damp undercoat in a cold hold.
