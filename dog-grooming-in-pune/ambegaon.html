Frequently asked questions

Are your groomers experienced with dogs that spend time outdoors?

Yes. Outdoor dogs often have more debris in their coats, higher tick exposure, and dirtier paws. We adjust our pre-session assessment and grooming protocol accordingly.

How thorough is the tick check?

We check all the high-risk areas — ears, neck, underbelly, between the toes, and around the tail. If we find anything, we tell you immediately.

Can I book on a monthly recurring basis?

Yes. Monthly recurring bookings are popular in Ambegaon particularly for outdoor-access dogs.

Is a large garden space suitable for the grooming setup?

A covered outdoor space or garden area works well for larger breeds. We do need water access and shelter from direct sun during the session.

What happens to the fur during and after the session?

We contain shed fur throughout the session and clean up completely afterward. The working area is left clean when we're done.

Is grooming suitable for dogs who haven't been groomed before?

Yes. We take extra time with first-time grooming experiences — introducing tools gradually and letting the dog set the pace early in the session.

Do you cover Nanded City, Kirkatwadi and the plots toward Sinhagad Road?

Yes, including the Narhe and Katraj side of Ambegaon. This belt is spread out with a lot of similar lane names, so send a location pin with the booking — it is the difference between the groomer arriving at your gate and calling you from somewhere near it.

We are on borewell water. Does hard water affect the coat?

It does. Hard water leaves mineral residue that dulls the coat and can leave skin itchy even after what feels like a proper rinse — a common complaint on this side of the city. Our groomers rinse for longer where the water is hard and finish with a conditioning rinse to counter it.

Street dogs come up to our compound. Does that matter for grooming?

It matters for what we inspect rather than what we do. Regular contact with free-ranging dogs raises the odds of ticks and skin issues, so the check at the start of the session is more thorough — between the toes, ears, jawline and under the tail. If we find something we tell you and show you where, rather than treating it silently.

Power cuts are frequent here. What happens if the electricity goes mid-session?

Only the dryer needs power, so a cut pauses the session rather than ending it. If you have an inverter, tell the groomer which sockets are on backup. What we will not do is leave a dog part-dried and go — if power is genuinely out we towel-dry thoroughly and wait for it rather than call the session finished.

Our dog spends most of the day outdoors on the plot. How often should we groom?

Every six to eight weeks for most outdoor-living coats, with a tick check every single session regardless of the gap. The bath frequency is a coat decision; the inspection frequency is not. Through monsoon we would tighten that toward six weeks and add shorter paw-hair trims so less mud comes indoors.
