Frequently asked questions

Do you service the hillside residential areas of Bavdhan?

Yes. We cover all residential areas in Bavdhan including those on the hillside developments.

How do you handle dogs with high outdoor activity and debris accumulation?

We do a coat condition assessment before starting and adjust our protocol accordingly. For debris-heavy coats, we include a pre-session dry brush to remove loose particles before the bath.

Can grooming be done in a garden or terrace space?

Yes. Covered outdoor spaces are excellent for grooming — particularly for drying. We need water access and some shelter from direct sun.

How do you manage tick checks for outdoor dogs?

Every session includes a thorough tick check covering ears, neck, underbelly, between toes, and the tail base. For high-risk outdoor dogs we use a mild anti-parasitic shampoo.

Is monthly grooming necessary for outdoor-access dogs?

For dogs with regular outdoor access in Bavdhan, monthly sessions are genuinely recommended — both for tick management and coat health.

What if my dog returns from a run just before the grooming appointment?

A slightly tired dog is often easier to groom. If they've come back muddy, we adjust the pre-wash protocol. Just let the groomer know when they arrive.

We hike with our dog on the hills here. Should we check for anything beyond ticks?

Grass seeds and burrs, which are the specific hazard of the Bavdhan and Chandni Chowk trails. They work between the toes and into the ear canal and can migrate under the skin if they are left. Our groomers check the webbing between every toe and inside both ears as standard, and it is one of the more valuable things a session does for a hill-walking dog.

What is a realistic slot given Chandni Chowk traffic?

Mid-morning or early afternoon. The Chandni Chowk approach at peak hours is one of the least predictable stretches in the city and we would rather book you into a window we can genuinely hold than commit to a 7 PM arrival we end up apologising for. If evening is your only option, expect a wider arrival window.

The rocky ground here is hard on paws. Do you do anything for that?

Every session includes a paw check — pads for cracks and abrasion, hair between the pads trimmed back so grit does not pack in, and nails taken to a length that lets the paw sit flat. On the hillside side of Bavdhan we see more pad wear than anywhere else we work, and a long nail on rocky ground changes how the whole foot loads.

Our dog is a large breed and we live on an upper floor without a lift. Does that work?

It works for us — the kit fits in one shoulder bag. The real question is your dog. If a heavy or older dog manages the stairs comfortably in daily life then nothing changes; if it does not, we would rather set up on the ground floor than have the dog do an unnecessary climb before standing still for an hour.

Our dog gets a lot of direct sun. Does that affect the coat?

It does — sustained sun exposure lightens and dries the guard hairs, and it is visible on dark coats in particular by the end of summer. A conditioning treatment during the session helps the coat hold moisture, and the more important rule is the one people get wrong: do not shave a double coat for the heat. The undercoat is what is shielding the skin from that sun.
