Frequently asked questions

My cat has never been groomed before — will this go okay?

First-time grooming sessions are always slower and more exploratory. We let the cat sniff the tools, get comfortable with the groomer, and we take breaks when needed. It won't be perfect but it will be a gentle introduction — far less overwhelming than a salon visit.

What shampoo do you use for cats?

We use pH-balanced cat-specific shampoos — not diluted dog shampoo, not human products. For cats with sensitive skin, flea history, or skin conditions, we carry appropriate alternatives. Just let us know when booking.

How do you handle a cat that bites or scratches?

We use low-restraint techniques and read the cat's body language carefully. If a cat is truly distressed we stop, allow them to settle, and resume. We never force a step. In some cases a full session may not be possible in one visit — we'll always communicate that honestly.

Does somebody have to be at home while you groom?

Yes, the pet owner or a trusted adult should be present throughout. This helps the cat feel more secure and means someone familiar can reassure them during tense moments.

How often does a cat need professional grooming?

Shorthair cats can go 8–10 weeks between sessions. Longhair breeds like Persians and Maine Coons ideally every 4–6 weeks to prevent matting. If your cat sheds heavily or goes outdoors, more frequent sessions help a lot.

Aircraft noise from the airport approach startles my cat. Does that affect the session?

It can, and it is worth mentioning at booking. Cats in Dhanori and Kalas live under the Lohegaon approach, and a low overflight during the dryer stage is two loud things at once for an animal already tolerating one. The groomer pauses through it rather than pushing on, and uses a lower fan setting for longer.

Litter tray granules get stuck in my cat's back fur. Is that something you deal with?

Routinely, and it is more common in longhairs than most owners realise. Clay and clumping granules bind into the fur around the hind legs and under the tail, and once wet they set hard. We comb them out before the bath rather than washing them in, and trimming the fur around the sanitary area is usually the fix that stops it recurring.

Should I take my cat's collar off before the groomer arrives?

Leave it on until the groomer is inside with the door shut, then it comes off for the session. A collar traps moisture against the neck during a bath and the fur underneath is where we most often find matting that owners have never seen. It goes back on before the groomer leaves.

Our water supply runs on fixed timings. Does a cat session need much?

Very little — a cat bath uses a fraction of what a dog needs, and two filled buckets cover a full session comfortably. If your supply is timed, keep those ready before the slot and tell the groomer on arrival so they work from stored water deliberately rather than finding the tap dry mid-rinse.
