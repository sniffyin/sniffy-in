Aircraft Noise, Sound-Sensitive Cats, and the Dryer

Lohegaon cats have opinions about loud noises. Fair enough.

Living under an approach path does something to a cat over time. Some Lohegaon cats stop noticing aircraft entirely and are the calmest animals we meet. Others go the other way — they become permanently primed for sudden loud noise, and by the time we arrive they are already running a little hot. We see both, sometimes in the same building.

The dryer is where this matters, because it is the one genuinely loud part of a grooming session and it is also the part we cannot skip. A cat sent away with a damp undercoat mats within days and, in a smaller or older cat, gets properly cold. So the answer is not to avoid drying — it is to change how we get there. We towel first and thoroughly, which removes far more water than most people manage and cuts the dryer time roughly in half. The dryer then goes on the lowest speed, is kept well away from the head, and is pointed along the body rather than into the coat. We build up to it rather than switching it on beside a cat who has not seen it.

For a cat that genuinely cannot tolerate it, we will towel-dry almost entirely and use the dryer for a short finishing pass on the densest areas only. Tell us at booking that your cat is sound-sensitive and the groomer plans for it from the start rather than discovering it halfway through.

Kittens, and Getting the First Year Right

What you do before six months decides the next fifteen years.

Lohegaon has a lot of young households and, consequently, a lot of kittens. This is the one part of a cat's life where grooming choices have compounding effects, so it is worth getting right.

A kitten that is handled early — paws touched, ears looked into, water run over its back, a dryer heard from across the room — grows into a cat that finds grooming unremarkable. A kitten that is left alone until it is two because it "doesn't need it yet" grows into a cat that has to be introduced to all of that as an adult with fully developed claws and firm opinions. The coat maintenance genuinely is not urgent in the first year. The habit is.

Practically: wait until the kitten is around twelve weeks and has settled in, and ideally leave a week or so after any vaccination. The first session is deliberately minimal — a warm, quick wash, claws, a look at the ears, and finished within about half an hour while the kitten is still finding it interesting rather than tedious. We will not attempt a full groom on a first visit even if the coat could take it, because the aim is a good memory, not a good result. From there, every six to eight weeks through the first year, and by the time your cat is fully grown the whole thing is just something that occasionally happens on a Tuesday.

Frequently asked questions

My cat hides whenever a stranger comes over — how will the session work?

This is very common. The groomer will give the cat time to emerge on their own terms. We never pull a cat out of hiding — that creates negative associations and makes things worse. Usually the cat comes out within 15–20 minutes when they realise the newcomer isn't threatening. From there, the session typically goes smoothly.

Can I stay in the room during the session?

Absolutely, and we prefer it. Having a familiar person nearby helps the cat feel safer, and you can reassure them during any tense moments. Some owners prefer to be in an adjacent room — either way is fine, as long as you're present in the home.

How long does a cat grooming session typically take?

Between 1.5 and 2.5 hours, depending on breed, coat condition, and how cooperative the cat is. Persian and longhair sessions tend to take longer than shorthair sessions. We never rush — the time it takes is the time it takes.

Is there a travel or visit charge for coming out to Lohegaon?

No. The package price is the same here as anywhere else we cover in Pune — ₹799 for a spa bath, up to ₹1,299 for a bath with a full haircut — and there is no doorstep fee, fuel surcharge or outer-area loading. Lohegaon is on our regular eastern route along with Dhanori and Viman Nagar, so it is not an exception trip for us. The only thing distance affects is scheduling: we give a slightly wider arrival window here because the airport road is unpredictable.

My kitten is teething and bites everything in reach. Will that be a problem?

It is normal and it is manageable, but do mention it. A teething kitten bites out of discomfort and curiosity rather than aggression, and hands moving near its face during a session are extremely tempting. The groomer will keep fingers out of range where possible, work with the kitten facing away from them for the fiddly parts, and give it something else to be interested in. What we will not do is scold or scruff for biting at this age — that teaches exactly the wrong lesson at exactly the wrong time.

Do you cover Vishrantwadi, Kalas and the Alandi Road side?

Yes — Lohegaon village, the societies off Airport Road, Kalas, Vishrantwadi, Tingre Nagar, Dhanori and the Alandi Road stretch are all part of the same route. If you are further out towards Charholi or Dighi, message us with a location pin first; we do go out that way but not every day, so it may mean waiting for a slot that fits the route.

We are directly under the flight path. Does that unsettle cats during grooming?

For sound-sensitive cats it can, particularly during the dryer stage when they are already tolerating one loud thing. Tell us at booking. The groomer will pause through an overflight rather than work on regardless, and use a lower fan setting for longer — slower, but it avoids stacking two stressors together.

There is open ground and dust around our society. Does an indoor cat still pick that up?

Yes. Dust from open plots drifts in and settles on the surfaces the cat sleeps on, and from there it goes into the coat and then gets swallowed during self-grooming. It is a fair reason to keep to the shorter end of the interval rather than stretching to the maximum your cat's coat type would otherwise allow.

Our society water supply is timed. Is that enough for a cat session?

Plenty. Two filled buckets cover the wash and rinse for a full cat session — considerably less than a dog needs. Keep them ready before the slot and tell the groomer on arrival so they plan the rinse from stored water rather than assuming a running tap.

Can we ask for a woman groomer for our cat?

Yes, just mention it at booking rather than on the day. Cats frequently respond differently to voice pitch and handling style, so it is a fair thing to plan around. The honest caveat is scheduling — it may mean a different day or a wider arrival window than your first-choice slot.

My cat hides under the bed the moment the doorbell rings. How do you get started?

We do not drag a cat out from under a bed. The groomer sits in the room, does nothing for several minutes, and lets the cat come out on its own — which most do once the door has been shut and nothing alarming has happened. If your cat has a favourite hiding spot, the practical help is shutting that room off shortly before the slot so they settle somewhere reachable.
