Why Yerawada Cats Are Better Groomed at Home

Narrow lanes, heavy traffic, and a carrier the cat already hates.

Yerawada traffic is among the worst in the city at any useful hour, and the older lanes are not somewhere anyone wants to walk a carrier. For a cat, that journey is the hardest part of the whole exercise — harder than the bath, the comb or the clippers. At-home cat grooming in Yerawada removes it entirely.

There is a second reason specific to this area. A lot of Yerawada cats are indoor-outdoor, or live in ground-floor homes with genuine street contact. Those cats carry a higher tick and flea load than sealed-tower cats, and a groomer working in your home checks the skin as part of every session rather than treating it as an extra.

Coat & skin care

Street Contact, Ticks and Cat-Safe Products

The one place where using the wrong product is genuinely dangerous.

Cats with outdoor access in Yerawada pick up ticks and fleas readily, particularly from June when warm wet weather suits them best. Every session here includes a real skin and ear check alongside the coat work.

What matters more than the check is what gets used afterwards. Permethrin-based tick products are ordinary for dogs and can be fatal to cats — this is the single most common serious mistake made by well-meaning owners buying something off a shelf. Everything we use on a cat is cat-formulated, and where what we are seeing looks like a veterinary problem rather than a grooming one we will tell you to see a vet instead of treating it ourselves.

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover Vishrantwadi, Shastri Nagar and the Kalyani Nagar edge?

Yes — Yerawada proper, Vishrantwadi, Shastri Nagar, the Gunjan Chowk side and the Kalyani Nagar edge, all at the same published prices.

Our lane is too narrow for a car. Is that a problem?

No. The groomer travels by two-wheeler with the whole kit in a bag. There is no van and nothing that needs to park outside.

My cat goes outside. Should I be worried about ticks and fleas?

It is worth a check every session, and we do one as standard. The important part is what gets used afterwards: permethrin-based tick products, which are routine for dogs, can be fatal to cats. Everything we use is cat-formulated, and where the problem looks veterinary rather than cosmetic we will tell you to see a vet rather than treating it.

I have an Indian shorthair. Does she need a full grooming package?

Usually not. For most healthy shorthairs the whole job is nails, ears, a comb-out and a coat and skin check — no bath required. We would rather tell you that than sell you a package. Where there is outdoor access, the tick check is the genuinely useful part.

My home is small. Is there enough room?

Almost certainly — cat sessions need less space than dog sessions. One room with a door that shuts, ideally a bathroom, is the entire requirement. The groomer works on his own mats.

The cat has never been groomed and is semi-feral. Will you still come?

Yes, and we will manage expectations honestly. First sessions with rescued or semi-feral cats are deliberately unambitious — often just nails and a partial comb-out — and we stop early rather than finish at any cost. No scruffing and no sedation.

Do you use dog products on cats if that is what is available?

Never, under any circumstances. Dog shampoo is unsafe on cats and dog tick treatments can kill them. The groomer carries cat-formulated products as standard rather than as an option.

Is a lion cut cruel for a matted Persian?

No — it is usually the kinder route. Brushing out a coat that has matted tight against the skin means an hour of pulling at the skin. Clipping is faster and less painful, and the coat grows back. We will tell you which situation you are in before starting.

How often should my cat be groomed?

Longhairs like Persians typically every 4 to 6 weeks in Pune humidity. Healthy Indian shorthairs often need only occasional nail and ear attention. We will tell you if you are booking more often than your cat needs.

How do I pay?

After the session, by cash or UPI, at the price quoted when you booked.
