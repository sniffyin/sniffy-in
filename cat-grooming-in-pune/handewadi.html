At your doorstep

At-Home Grooming Is Simply Better for Cats

The science behind why familiar environments matter.

Cats have a fundamentally different relationship with territory than dogs. Their home is not just a comfortable place — it's their psychological anchor. Removing them from it causes stress responses that remain elevated throughout a salon visit and often for hours to days afterward. In-home grooming means the cat's cortisol levels are lower from the start, they can retreat to familiar spots between steps, and they have the option to approach the groomer on their own terms. This produces calmer sessions and better grooming outcomes.

Grooming frequency

How Often Does a Cat Actually Need Grooming?

The honest answer depends on the coat, not the calendar.

This is the question we get asked most in Handewadi, usually by people who have just had a first session and want to know when to book the next one. There is no single right number, but there is a sensible way to work it out, and it comes down to coat type plus how much your cat can still do for itself.

A short-coated indoor cat in good health genuinely manages on a session every eight to ten weeks — at that point it is claws, ears and a deshedding pass rather than urgent coat work. A semi-longhair such as a Ragdoll or Maine Coon needs six to eight weeks, because the areas that mat first are the ones the cat cannot reach. A Persian or heavily coated cat needs four to six, and daily brushing in between; anything longer and we are de-matting rather than grooming. Then adjust for the cat rather than the breed: an overweight cat that cannot twist to reach its back, a senior with stiff joints, or one recovering from illness all need shorter gaps regardless of coat length. Handewadi's larger homes and garden access also push the interval down a little, because outdoor time means more in the coat.

If you are not sure where your cat falls, ask the groomer at the end of the session. They will have just had their hands on the whole animal and will give you a number rather than a sales pitch.

Coat & skin care

The Sanitary Trim, and Why Nobody Mentions It

Not glamorous. Genuinely important.

Every full session includes a sanitary trim, and it is the part of the service almost no owner asks about in advance and almost every owner is grateful for afterwards. It means neatening the fur immediately around the rear and, on longhaired cats, the fur on the back of the thighs.

The reason it matters is simple and slightly unpleasant. On a longhaired cat, waste catches in the fur around the rear and dries there. The cat tries to clean it, cannot reach properly, and either ingests it or leaves it. Left long enough it forms a hard plug of matted fur that blocks the area entirely, which is a genuine medical emergency and one of the more distressing things we occasionally find. The same problem appears on overweight cats and on older cats who have stopped being able to twist round, regardless of coat length.

A trim of a few centimetres, done every session, prevents all of it. We keep it modest and tidy rather than shaved, so it is not visible on a standing cat. If we find the area already clogged, we will clip it out carefully and tell you what we found, because it usually means the cat needs either a shorter grooming interval or a look from a vet.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get my cat used to being groomed at home for the first time?

In the days before the session, leave the grooming area accessible so the cat can investigate it. Don't change their routine significantly the day of the session. During the session, stay present and calm — your energy affects your cat's state. Don't intervene unless asked, but being there helps the cat feel safer.

Can you groom two cats in the same household back to back?

Yes, we can do back-to-back sessions in the same home. It helps to separate the cats during each other's sessions so the grooming smells and sounds of one don't unsettle the other. The second cat often benefits from hearing that the first session went fine.

What areas near Handewadi do you also cover?

We cover Handewadi, Undri, Mohammadwadi, NIBM Road, and surrounding pockets of south-east Pune. Message us with your pincode if you're unsure about coverage.

My cat brings up a hairball two or three times a month. Is that normal?

Occasional hairballs are normal; two or three a month is on the high side and usually points at loose undercoat the cat is swallowing during self-grooming. Regular deshedding removes that fur before it ends up inside the cat, and most owners see the frequency drop noticeably once they settle into a routine. What is not normal is repeated retching with nothing coming up, or a cat that goes off food alongside it — that needs a vet the same week, because a hairball that is not coming up in either direction is a serious problem.

Do you charge more for a longhaired cat than a shorthaired one?

The package price is the same — sessions start at ₹799 regardless of coat length. Where the cost can change is if your cat needs a haircut rather than just a bath, which moves it up the package list, or if there is significant de-matting to work through. In that case the groomer will tell you what is involved and what it will cost before starting, not after. A longhaired cat on a regular schedule almost never runs into that; a longhaired cat left for eight months usually does.

My cat sprays indoors. Will grooming help with the smell?

Only a little, and we would rather be straight with you about it. Bathing removes odour from the coat itself, so a cat that has sprayed and then walked through it will smell better afterwards. But spraying is territorial behaviour, not a hygiene problem, and it will continue regardless of how clean the cat is. The things that actually change it are neutering, reducing stress or competition between cats in the house, and enzymatic cleaning of the marked spots so the cat stops being drawn back to them. Your vet is the right person for that conversation.

Do you cover Undri, Mohammed Wadi and the Saswad Road side?

Yes, along with the Handewadi Road belt and the Kondhwa side. The area has grown quickly and several societies share builder names across phases, so mention your phase or tower at booking and send a pin — it saves the groomer a call from your gate.

Highway dust is constant here. Does that reach an indoor cat?

Yes. Road dust off the Pune–Solapur stretch drifts indoors and settles on the surfaces the cat sleeps on, and from there it goes into the coat and gets swallowed during self-grooming. It is a fair reason to keep to the shorter end of your cat's interval rather than stretching it.

Our new society still has irregular water. Is that enough for a cat session?

Easily — two filled buckets cover the wash and rinse for a full session. If your supply is timed, either book inside that window or keep buckets ready and tell the groomer on arrival so they work from stored water deliberately rather than mid-lather.

Are weekend slots available?

Yes, Saturday and Sunday at the same 9 AM to 9 PM hours. Weekends are the busiest window in this belt because most households here work a standard week, so book four or five days ahead rather than trying on the Friday.

My cat hides the moment the doorbell rings. How does the session start?

We do not pull a cat out of a hiding place. The groomer sits in the closed room and does nothing for the first few minutes, letting the cat emerge on its own once nothing alarming has happened — which most do. The practical help from your side is shutting off the favourite hiding spot shortly before the slot so the cat settles somewhere reachable.
