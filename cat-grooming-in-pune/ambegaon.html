Services explained

Every Cat Is Different. We Adapt Fully.

No two sessions are the same.

A fluffy Persian in a peaceful Ambegaon apartment and a rescue Indie who lived outdoors until last year need completely different approaches to grooming. Our groomers arrive without assumptions and assess each cat individually — coat type, temperament, grooming history, and any special considerations. The session that emerges from that assessment is appropriate for that specific cat, not a generic procedure applied to every animal we see.

At your doorstep

Bigger Homes, More Cats — Ambegaon Households Rarely Stop at One

Multi-cat families are the norm here, not the exception.

Ambegaon Budruk and the plots towards Narhe have space that central Pune does not, and space is how people end up with three cats instead of one. A stray kitten turns up at the gate, someone fosters a litter and keeps two, a neighbour moves away and leaves theirs. Most of our Ambegaon bookings are for more than one cat.

Grooming a multi-cat household is a different exercise from grooming a single cat, and the order matters. We take the most confident cat first, because the others will be watching and a calm first session sets the tone for the rest. The groomed cat then goes into a separate room — not because of anything dramatic, but because a freshly washed cat smells wrong to its housemates for a few hours and that is where the hissing comes from. We also change the water and clean the combs between cats, which sounds obvious and is skipped surprisingly often; ringworm and ear mites move between cats in exactly this way. Book them together and the whole household is done in one visit rather than three.

Cat Breeds We Groom Around Ambegaon

Mostly rescues, and increasingly not only rescues.

Ambegaon's cat population leans heavily towards Indies and rescues, which suits us — they are the cats most often skipped by grooming services and the ones who most often need a proper look-over. The newer societies have started bringing in pedigrees too. Here is how we handle the common ones:

  • Indie and rescue cat grooming in Ambegaon — Frequently a first professional session at the age of five or six. We expect a flea check to be necessary, we expect the nails to be overdue, and we expect the cat to object. None of that is a problem; we just plan a longer, gentler first visit.
  • Persian cat grooming in Ambegaon — The dry south-Pune air plus construction dust is a hard combination for this coat. Detangle first, wash second, and dry completely — a Persian sent away damp will mat within a week.
  • Domestic shorthair and tabby grooming in Ambegaon — Straightforward coats where the real value is deshedding and nails. Also the group where we most often spot early skin issues, because there is nowhere for anything to hide on a short coat.
  • Ragdoll grooming in Ambegaon — Increasingly common in the newer buildings. Famously docile, which tempts owners into skipping grooming because the cat never complains. The silky coat mats behind the legs quietly; we check there first.
  • Kittens and young cats — With so many rescued litters in this area, a lot of our Ambegaon work is first sessions for cats under a year. Short, warm, positive, and finished before the kitten gets bored.

Rescue, pedigree or complete mystery — bring us the cat and we will work out the coat.

Services explained

The Part of the Session Nobody Books For

A groomer sees your cat's whole body. That turns out to matter.

You live with your cat and you love your cat, and you still almost certainly have not run your hands over every square inch of it in the last two months. A groomer does exactly that, every session, in good light, with the coat wet and flat against the skin. It is the closest thing to a monthly physical your cat gets between vet visits.

What surfaces is rarely dramatic and often useful: a scab under the chin that turns out to be feline acne from a plastic food bowl, a warm swollen toe from a claw growing into the pad, dandruff concentrated along the spine on a cat too stiff to reach back there, a lump that was not there last time, wax deep in one ear and not the other. We are groomers, not vets — we do not diagnose and we will never sell you a treatment. What we do is tell you clearly what we found, where it is, and whether we think it is worth a vet's opinion. Several Ambegaon clients have caught things early this way, which is a better argument for regular grooming than a shiny coat ever was.

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover Narhe and surrounding areas near Ambegaon?

Yes — Ambegaon, Narhe, Sus Road, and adjacent pockets of south-west Pune are all covered. If you're unsure about your specific location, message us and we'll confirm.

My cat has never had its nails clipped — is that okay to do in the first session?

Nail trimming is part of every standard session. For cats who've never had their nails clipped, we approach it slowly — letting the cat get used to having their paws handled before using the clippers. It may not be the fastest nail trim but it gets done without trauma.

Can you groom a cat that's currently nursing kittens?

We advise waiting until the kittens are fully weaned before a full grooming session. The stress of grooming during nursing can affect milk production and the mother cat's state. A very gentle nail and ear check is usually fine, but a full bath and blow-dry session is best postponed.

We have four cats. Can they all be done in one visit, and how does pricing work?

Yes, and it is the sensible way to do it — one visit, one setup, and the household settles back to normal the same evening. Each cat is priced as its own session starting at ₹799, so four cats means four sessions rather than a bulk rate, but you save the repeated travel and disruption. Tell us the number when booking so we allocate a long enough slot; four cats usually runs three to four hours.

My cat is quite overweight and can't reach his own back to groom himself. Does that change the session?

It changes it a lot, and it is a common situation in homes with free-fed indoor cats. A cat who cannot twist round to reach the base of the spine and the tail area will develop dandruff, clumped greasy fur and sometimes mats in exactly those spots, plus a sanitary area that needs attention because he cannot clean it properly either. We spend most of the session on the back half, keep him lying on his side rather than asking him to sit up, and go easy on the handling since heavier cats tire faster. Regular sessions matter more for these cats than for any other kind.

If the groomer finds something on my cat's skin, will you treat it?

No — we will show you and describe it, and that is where our role stops. Our groomers are trained to notice things, not to diagnose them, and we do not carry or sell medication. If we find a scab, a lump, an inflamed ear or anything that looks like a fungal patch, we will point out exactly where it is, avoid working that area, and suggest you have a vet look. If it looks like it needs attention today rather than next week, we will say so plainly.

Do you cover Nanded City, Kirkatwadi and the Katraj side of Ambegaon?

Yes, including Narhe and the plotted developments toward Sinhagad Road. It is a spread-out belt with a lot of similar lane names, so send a location pin with the booking — it is the difference between the groomer reaching your gate and calling you from near it.

Does borewell water suit a cat’s skin, or should we use stored water?

Either works — what matters is the rinse, not the source. Hard borewell water leaves mineral deposit that dulls the coat and can leave skin flaky, and a cat then ingests that residue when she self-grooms afterwards. We rinse for noticeably longer on this side of the city and finish with a conditioning rinse, which clears most of it.

Power cuts happen often here. What if the electricity goes mid-session?

Only the drying stage needs power, so a cut pauses the session rather than ending it. Tell the groomer which sockets are on your inverter if you have one. A cat left damp is not an outcome we will accept — if power is genuinely out we towel-dry thoroughly, keep the room warm and wait.

Our cat spends the day in the compound. How often should she be groomed?

Every four to six weeks for a longhair with outdoor access, six to eight for a shorthair — but with a tick check at every single session regardless of the gap. The bathing interval is a coat decision; the inspection interval is not. Through monsoon we would tighten both.

There are street cats around our plot. Should I worry about disease?

It is worth being aware of rather than alarmed by. Cats that meet free-ranging cats face higher exposure to fleas, ear mites and skin infections, all of which we check for at the start of every session and flag to you. What we cannot do is diagnose or treat — if we find something that looks like more than a grooming issue, we tell you to see your vet.
