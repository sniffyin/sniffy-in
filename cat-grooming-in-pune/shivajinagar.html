We're Cat Groomers, Not Dog Groomers Who Accept Cats

The difference matters a lot.

Most Pune salons are fundamentally dog grooming operations that also take cat bookings. The products, the handling training, the restraint methods — all designed for dogs. Cats require completely different approaches: lower restraint, reading body language signals, different shampoo chemistry, and far more patience with session pacing. No Cage, No Restraint Bag, No Scruffing

How we actually hold a cat, and what we refuse to do.

Walk into a busy grooming setup anywhere and you will see the standard toolkit for a difficult cat: a mesh restraint bag, a scruff hold, a cage dryer to leave them in while the groomer works on something else. We use none of the three, and it is worth explaining why rather than just claiming to be gentler.

Scruffing — gripping the loose skin at the back of the neck — works on a kitten because a queen carries her litter that way. On an adult cat it does not induce calm; it induces immobility through fear, and the cat remembers. A restraint bag has the same problem at greater scale: the cat cannot move at all, so it stops resisting, and everyone mistakes shutdown for cooperation. Cage dryers are worse again, because the cat is confined with a noise it cannot escape and nobody is watching it closely.

What we do instead is slower and, in a home, entirely workable. The cat is on a non-slip mat rather than a slippery table, so it is not bracing against falling the whole time. One hand rests on the body for steadiness rather than gripping. We work from the side, never looming over the head. When the cat tenses we stop and wait a few seconds rather than pushing through — and that pause, repeated a dozen times across a session, is most of what separates a calm groom from a fight. In Shivajinagar, where flats are compact and there is nowhere to take a cat away to, this is the only approach that actually works.

The 24 Hours After the Groomer Leaves

What is normal, and what is worth a phone call.

Nobody warns first-time clients about the evening after a grooming session, so people worry unnecessarily. Here is what usually happens.

Your cat will almost certainly re-groom itself thoroughly for the next hour or two. This looks like an insult and is actually just a cat putting its own scent back on a coat that now smells faintly of shampoo. Some cats sulk under a bed for an hour. Some are unusually affectionate. A few are noticeably more active, which is a real effect — a cat carrying less dead undercoat is genuinely more comfortable. If you have more than one cat, expect some hissing at the newly washed one for a few hours until the household smell settles; it passes on its own and is not a sign of anything wrong.

What is not normal: refusing food the next day, hiding for longer than a few hours in a cat that is not usually a hider, limping, repeated head-shaking after an ear clean, or any redness on the skin. None of those are common, and all of them are worth telling us about and mentioning to your vet. We would far rather hear that something seemed off than have you assume it was just the grooming. Message us the next day either way if you are unsure — the groomer will remember your cat and will tell you honestly whether what you are describing is expected.

Frequently asked questions

Can your groomers spot health issues during the session?

Our groomers are trained to notice common signs of health concerns during grooming — skin irritation, ear redness, unusual lumps, overgrown or ingrown nails, and matting patterns that might indicate underlying issues. We'll flag anything we notice, but we're groomers, not vets — a vet visit is the next step for anything we flag.

Is home grooming hygienic for my other family members?

Yes. We use clean equipment for each session, wipe down the grooming area before and after, and all waste (shed fur, nail clippings) is bagged and taken away. We can work in any area of the home — bathroom, kitchen, or on a waterproof mat in any room.

My cat is 12 years old — is grooming still okay at that age?

Senior cats actually benefit more from regular grooming because they groom themselves less effectively as they age. We adapt the session for older cats — slower pace, softer handling, shorter segments with more breaks. Age alone isn't a reason to skip grooming; in fact it's a reason to continue it.

Our lane is narrow and there is nowhere to park. Is that a problem?

Not really — this is central Pune and we expect it. Our groomers travel by two-wheeler with the kit in a single bag precisely because half of Shivajinagar, Deccan and the FC Road lanes cannot take a car. What does help is a clear landmark rather than an address: the building name, the shop on the corner, which side of the lane. Google's pin is frequently wrong in the older parts of this area, so a WhatsApp location plus one sentence of directions saves everybody ten minutes.

My cat is deaf. Does that change how the session is handled?

Yes, and it is useful to tell us in advance. A deaf cat cannot hear the groomer approaching, so being touched unexpectedly is genuinely startling in a way it would not be for a hearing cat. The groomer will make sure to come into her line of sight before making contact, use firmer and more predictable hand pressure rather than light touches, and let her see the clipper and the dryer before either is switched on. The dryer itself is often less of an issue for a deaf cat than a hearing one — it is the vibration and the airflow they react to, not the noise.

Do you work on Sundays and public holidays?

Yes, seven days a week from 9 AM to 9 PM, holidays included. Sundays are our busiest day across central Pune, so a Sunday slot in Shivajinagar generally needs four or five days' notice. If you want a specific time rather than whatever is left, book earlier in the week. Weekday mornings here are usually available at a day's notice.

Do you cover the older wada buildings in central Shivajinagar?

Yes, and they are a normal address for us. What matters is one room that can be shut, a tap and a plug point — not modern plumbing. If the only usable socket is a long way from where your cat is comfortable, mention it and the groomer will set up around the cat rather than move the cat to the socket.

Our lane gets very noisy with traffic and horns. Does that unsettle a cat mid-session?

Most central Shivajinagar cats are thoroughly habituated to it, so usually no. For a cat that genuinely is reactive, we work in the room furthest from the road with the windows shut, and use the dryer on its lowest setting for longer rather than a faster, louder dry.

Can I book just a nail trim without the full session?

Yes — a short maintenance visit for nails and an ear clean is available below the full session price. Ask for it by name. For an indoor shorthair it is often all that is genuinely needed between full grooms, and for an elderly owner who cannot safely trim a squirming cat it is the most useful thing we do.

My cat is blind. Does that change how the session runs?

It changes the approach rather than the outcome. A blind cat relies on scent and sound, so the groomer announces themselves by touch before every step instead of reaching in silently, keeps the tools in one place, and does not move the cat around the room unnecessarily. Blind cats often groom very calmly, because a session at home is on ground they already know.

How does grooming work during Ganpati week here?

Book early and, if you can, book before the festival rather than during it. Processions close the central lanes at short notice and we cannot hold arrival windows reliably. For a sound-sensitive cat, a session on a night with drumming outside is asking them to cope with two stressful things at once.
