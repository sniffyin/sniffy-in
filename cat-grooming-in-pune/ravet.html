A Lot of Ravet Cat Parents Are Doing This for the First Time

New flats, new families, first cat.

Ravet filled up fast, mostly with young families buying their first home. A cat often arrives in year two, from a litter someone at work was rehoming or from a rescue group in Pimpri. Which means a large share of the cat parents we meet here are genuinely new to it, and nobody has told them what grooming is actually for.

The honest version is short. Cats groom themselves well enough to stay presentable and not well enough to stay healthy. What they cannot do is trim a claw that is curving towards the pad, remove the dead undercoat sitting near the skin, clear wax from deep in an ear, or reach the base of their own spine once they put on a little weight. Those four things are most of what a professional session is. Everything else — the shine, the smell, the way the coat sits — is a pleasant side effect. If you are booking your first session and are not sure what to ask for, say so; the groomer will walk you through what your particular cat needs and, just as usefully, what it does not.

What a Deshedding Session Actually Is

It is not a haircut, and it is not shaving.

Deshedding is the step most first-time owners in Ravet have heard of and almost nobody has had explained, so here it is. A cat's coat has two layers — the outer guard hairs you can see, and a soft undercoat underneath. The undercoat is what dies off and loosens seasonally. Because it sits close to the skin, a normal brush glides over the top of it without ever making contact.

A deshedding session removes that layer deliberately: a dry brush-out first, then a shampoo that helps release the dead coat while the cat is wet, then a fine-toothed comb worked right down to the skin once the coat is fully dry. Nothing is cut and nothing is shaved — the cat leaves with the same coat length it arrived with, just without a fistful of dead fur in it. The visible results are less fur on your furniture, fewer hairballs, and a coat that lies flat instead of standing away from the body. On a heavy shedder it is the single most worthwhile part of the whole session.

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover Akurdi and Chinchwad Station area near Ravet?

How do I tell if my cat has a mat that needs professional attention?

Mats feel like hard, compressed clumps in the coat — they're different from just tangled fur and can't be combed out with a regular brush. They're most common near the neck (under the collar), armpits, inner legs, and belly. If you feel something that doesn't move or brush out easily, that's a mat that needs professional attention.

What products do you use and are they Indian formulations?

We use a mix of quality domestic and imported cat-specific shampoos and conditioners — all pH-balanced for feline skin, free from common irritants. We don't use human products or dog-specific products on cats. Specific brand information is available on request.

Will grooming stop my cat shedding completely?

No, and be wary of anyone who says it will. Shedding is a normal, continuous process and a healthy cat will always lose coat. What a deshedding session does is remove the dead undercoat in one controlled go instead of letting it come out gradually all over your flat. Expect a clear drop in loose fur for roughly two to three weeks, then a gradual return as new coat loosens. Cats who shed heavily usually settle into a four to six week rhythm; light shedders manage on eight.

Yes to both, and they are the first to go. We run from 9 AM to 9 PM seven days a week, and the after-work slots from around 6 PM plus the whole of Saturday and Sunday are the busiest in the Ravet-Akurdi belt because so many households here have two people working full time. Booking four or five days ahead usually gets you the slot you actually want; weekday mornings are almost always available at short notice.

Just approve the groomer the way you would any other visitor. Once the booking is confirmed we send you the groomer's name and mobile number, so you can add them in advance or accept the entry request when it pops up. Most of the newer Ravet towers work this way and it is not a problem — the only thing that costs time is nobody being reachable when the groomer is standing at the gate, so keep your phone nearby around the start of the slot.

New townships here have irregular water supply. How do we plan?

Keep two filled buckets ready before the slot if your supply is timed — that covers the wash and rinse for a full cat session with room to spare. Tell the groomer on arrival so they plan the rinse deliberately rather than discovering the tap is dry mid-lather.

Someone in the family has developed a cat allergy. Does grooming help enough?

It helps meaningfully but it does not solve it, and you deserve the honest version. The allergen is a protein in saliva and skin secretions that dries onto the coat and sheds into the air on loose fur — so regular bathing and deshedding removes a real share of it. It manages the problem; it does not eliminate it. Combine sessions with an air purifier and keeping the bedroom cat-free.

Our tower has a service lift and a separate entrance. Does that complicate things?

No — the groomer follows whatever your building requires and carries everything in one bag. The only thing worth flagging at booking is if the service entrance is on a different side of the complex from the main gate, which is where a groomer usually loses ten minutes in a large Ravet township.

What happens to a half-dried cat if the power cuts out?

The session pauses; it does not end. Only the drying stage needs electricity, so tell the groomer which sockets run off your inverter if you have one. We will not leave a cat damp — a wet coat in Pune humidity stays wet at the skin for hours, so we towel-dry thoroughly, keep the room warm and wait for power rather than calling it finished.
