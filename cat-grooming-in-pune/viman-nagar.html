Services explained

Bathing the Cat Yourself vs Booking a Session

An honest comparison, including where we lose.

Plenty of Viman Nagar cat parents bathe their own cats, and we are not going to pretend that is wrong. It is free, it happens on your schedule, and for a calm shorthaired cat who tolerates water it is perfectly adequate maintenance. If that describes your situation, keep doing it.

Where a home bath falls short is not the washing — it is everything around it. Almost nobody at home does a dry brush-out first, so loose fur turns into a wet mat during the bath rather than coming off before it. Almost nobody rinses long enough, which leaves residue that dulls the coat within days. Most people cannot dry a cat completely by themselves, and a coat that is dry on top and damp underneath is the single most reliable way to produce mats. And the parts that need a second pair of hands — nails on a squirming cat, an ear clean, a sanitary trim, working a mat loose without pulling skin — are the ones that quietly get skipped year after year.

The other difference is that a groomer is looking. Bathing your own cat, you are focused on the logistics; you are not systematically checking each claw, parting the fur along the spine, or comparing one ear to the other. Most of what we find, we find because finding it is the job rather than a side effect.

The sensible middle ground, and what a lot of our Viman Nagar clients settle on: brush at home regularly, bathe yourself if you and the cat both cope with it, and book a professional session every couple of months for the parts that need equipment, technique and a second person.

Coat & skin care

The Skin Problems We See Most Often

Three common ones, and which of them grooming actually helps.

A wet coat pressed flat against the skin shows you things a dry coat hides completely, which is why so much of what we report back to owners is skin rather than fur. Three conditions come up again and again in this part of the city.

Flea allergy dermatitis is the most common by a distance. It looks like a scattering of small scabs along the lower back and around the base of the tail, and it is not caused by the number of fleas — a single bite can set off a reaction in a sensitised cat, which is why owners insist there are no fleas and are usually right. Grooming and an anti-flea bath help; they do not resolve it, because the treatment has to cover the cat and the home together.

Feline acne is the second, and it surprises people every time. Small black specks or crusty bumps on the chin, often mistaken for dirt. The usual culprit is a plastic food or water bowl that harbours bacteria in surface scratches. Switching to steel or ceramic and washing it daily fixes most cases; grooming keeps the area clean while it settles.

And dandruff, particularly concentrated along the spine where the cat cannot reach. In a young cat that usually means dry indoor air or a diet short on fatty acids. In an older or overweight cat it more often means the cat physically cannot twist round to groom back there, which is a comfort problem worth taking seriously. Regular deshedding and a conditioning wash genuinely help this one.

In all three cases we describe what we see, avoid working an inflamed area, and tell you whether we think it needs a vet. We do not diagnose and we do not sell treatments.

Frequently asked questions

My cat has a health condition — can she still be groomed?

In most cases yes, with adaptation. Let us know the condition when booking and we'll discuss whether any modifications are needed. For cats on chemotherapy, post-surgery, or with very fragile skin conditions, we recommend a vet clearance first.

Do you groom elderly cats (10+ years)?

Yes, and we strongly encourage it. Senior cats groom themselves less effectively and their coats can deteriorate significantly if professional grooming stops. We adapt our approach for older cats — softer handling, slower sessions, and extra care for arthritic joints or sensitive areas.

Can you pick up after the grooming — shed fur, nail clippings?

Yes, clean-up is part of the service. We bag and remove all grooming waste before leaving. The grooming area will be clean and dry when we're done.

Is it a bad idea to bathe my cat myself?

Not at all, if she tolerates it and you use a cat-specific shampoo. Two things to get right, because they are where home baths usually go wrong. Brush the coat out thoroughly while it is dry, before any water — wetting a coat with loose fur in it turns that fur into mats. And rinse far longer than feels necessary, then dry completely, including the undercoat; a coat that is dry on the surface and damp underneath will knot within days. What we would not attempt alone are nails on an unwilling cat, ear cleaning and de-matting, all of which really do need two hands and the right tools.

My cat has small scabs along her lower back near the tail. What is that?

That pattern — scattered small scabs concentrated on the lower back and the base of the tail — is the classic presentation of flea allergy dermatitis. The frustrating part is that it does not take an infestation; a cat that has become sensitised can react strongly to a single bite, which is why owners are often certain there are no fleas and are technically correct. An anti-flea bath during the session helps and we will check the coat carefully, but resolving it properly means treating both the cat and the home, and that is a plan to make with your vet.

We are in a serviced apartment on a short lease. Do you come to those?

Yes — serviced apartments, rented flats, guest houses and long-stay accommodation are all fine, and Viman Nagar has plenty of all four. There is nothing about our setup that requires a permanent home: we need a bathroom with a working tap, a plug point and floor space for a mat, and we take everything away afterwards. If the building requires visitors to be registered at reception, tell us at booking and we will send the groomer's details in advance.

What slot avoids the worst of the Phoenix and Nagar Road traffic?

Mid-morning, roughly 10 AM to 12:30, or mid-afternoon. The stretch past the mall between 6 and 9 PM is where arrival windows slip most. If evening is your only option we would rather book you at 7 PM with an honest window than promise a precise time we cannot hold.

Our society will not permit grooming or drying on balconies. Does that limit anything?

Not at all — the entire session happens indoors as standard, in one room with the door shut. We would never air-dry a cat on a balcony anyway: Pune humidity keeps an air-dried coat damp at the skin for hours, which is how fungal patches begin, and an open balcony is the single riskiest place for a startled cat.

My cat has a microchip. Should I mention it?

Worth mentioning, mainly so you know we will not be scanning or touching it — the chip sits under the skin between the shoulder blades and is entirely unaffected by bathing or clipping. If your cat is not chipped, the more useful takeaway is that a home session involves a stranger and an opening door, and an unchipped cat that bolts is very hard to recover.

Can you groom two cats in the same visit if they do not get along?

Yes, and that is the normal arrangement rather than the exception. Each cat is groomed alone in a closed room while the other stays elsewhere in the house. Cats read each other's stress sharply, and two cats that already have friction will make each other's sessions worse if they can see or hear one another.

We are in a serviced apartment on a short lease. Is grooming still practical?

Yes, and it is a common booking in this area. The groomer works on their own mats, contains the shed fur and clippings, and leaves the bathroom as they found it — which matters more in a leased flat than an owned one. Tell us at booking if the building requires the management office to approve service visitors.
