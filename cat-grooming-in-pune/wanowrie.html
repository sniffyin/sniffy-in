Services explained

Grooming a Cat With a Diagnosed Condition

Kidney disease, thyroid trouble, arthritis — the session changes.

Wanowrie has a lot of cats in the second half of their lives, which means a lot of cats managing something. Chronic kidney disease and hyperthyroidism are the two most common diagnoses in older cats, and both show up in the coat long before anyone books a grooming session about them — a dull, greasy or unkempt coat in a cat who used to be fastidious is one of the earliest visible signs of both.

These cats need grooming more than a healthy cat does, because they have largely stopped doing it themselves. But the session has to be built differently. A kidney-compromised cat loses heat quickly and dehydrates easily, so we keep the water warm, work faster, and dry thoroughly rather than letting a damp cat sit. A hyperthyroid cat is often restless and thin with fragile skin, so handling is lighter and shorter. An arthritic cat is groomed lying on its side, in short blocks, with the positions that require twisting simply avoided.

The practical part is on you: tell us the diagnosis and any medication when you book. Not because we treat any of it — we do not — but because it tells the groomer what to plan for and what to leave alone, and because it changes what counts as a good session. For a fourteen-year-old with CKD, forty comfortable minutes covering claws, sanitary area and a gentle wash is a complete success. Pushing on for a flawless finish is not.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal for a cat to start grooming themselves immediately after a professional session?

Yes, and it's actually a positive sign. Cats self-groom after handling to restore their own scent and "sort out" how they smell. If your cat starts grooming soon after the session, it generally means they're relaxed and comfortable, not distressed. A cat that retreats and doesn't groom for hours may still be processing the experience.

My cat is currently on antibiotics. Should I wait to book?

In most cases, grooming during antibiotic treatment is fine — the bath won't affect the medication. If the antibiotics are for a skin condition or wound, wait until the area has healed sufficiently. When in doubt, check with your vet.

How do you handle payment?

Payment is at the end of the session by cash or UPI. We don't collect advance payment — you pay only after the session is complete and you're satisfied with the work.

Do you cover Salunke Vihar, Undri and the Mohammadwadi side?

Yes — Wanowrie, Salunke Vihar, Undri, Mohammadwadi, NIBM Road, Kondhwa and the Hadapsar border are all on the same south-east route. Most of the societies here need the groomer registered at the gate, so let us know at booking and we will send their name and number in advance. It is a well-covered corridor for us, so mid-week slots are often available at a day or two's notice.

My cat has been diagnosed with kidney disease. Is bathing still safe for her?

Generally yes, and she probably needs it more than she used to, since cats with CKD tend to stop grooming themselves properly. What changes is how we do it. A kidney-compromised cat loses body heat faster and tires sooner, so we keep the water warm, work in shorter blocks, dry thoroughly rather than leaving her damp, and finish earlier than we would with a healthy cat. Tell us the diagnosis and any medication when you book so the groomer plans for it. If she is in a flare or your vet has advised against handling for now, wait.

My longhaired cat mats again within a month of every session. Is that normal?

It is common, and it usually means one of two things. Either the interval is genuinely too long for that coat — some Persians and Maine Coons need four weeks rather than six or eight in Pune's humidity — or the coat is not being combed at home between visits, in which case no professional interval will keep up. Worth checking where the mats keep forming: if it is always the same spot, that is often a place the cat cannot reach and you are not combing. Ask your groomer to point out the three or four trouble areas on your particular cat and concentrate your five minutes a day there.

Is entry to the cantonment-side addresses here straightforward?

Yes, with a little notice. The groomer carries photo identification and we can send their name and number ahead so your gate can log the entry before the slot. These addresses are stricter about pre-notification than the civilian societies, and a groomer held at a barrier is time lost from the cat’s session.

Do you need power for the whole cat session?

Only for the drying stage — everything else is hand work. If your building has an inverter, tell the groomer which sockets are backed up. A cut pauses the session rather than ending it, and we will not leave a cat damp: we towel-dry down thoroughly, keep the room warm and wait rather than finish early.

Is it harder to get a slot around Diwali?

Considerably, and there is a second reason to book early. Slots across Wanowrie and NIBM go about a week ahead in festival weeks — but more importantly, fireworks and a stranger with a dryer on the same evening is a great deal to ask of a cat. Book before the festival rather than during it.

Can you groom two cats that do not get along in the same visit?

Yes, and separately is the standard arrangement rather than the exception. Each cat is groomed alone in a closed room while the other stays elsewhere in the house. Cats read each other's stress sharply, and two that already have friction will make each other's sessions worse if they can see or hear one another.

Should I feed my cat before the groomer arrives?

A light meal two or three hours ahead, and nothing immediately before. A full stomach combined with handling and being turned over makes some cats nauseous, while a very hungry cat is restless and slow to settle. Feed properly once the session is finished and the coat is dry.
