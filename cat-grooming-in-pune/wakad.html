Cat Allergies in a Sealed, Air-Conditioned Flat

Grooming will not cure it. It does change the load.

This comes up more in Wakad than anywhere else we work, and it is usually the same conversation: someone in the household has developed a reaction, the flat is a modern sealed unit with the AC running most of the year, and the family is quietly wondering whether they can keep the cat.

The first thing worth knowing is that people are not allergic to cat hair. The trigger is a protein the cat produces in its saliva and skin oils, which dries onto the coat during self-grooming and then travels on flakes of skin — dander — that are small enough to stay airborne for hours. In a sealed air-conditioned flat with the same air recirculating, that dander does not settle and disperse the way it would in a ventilated house. It accumulates. Which is why the reaction often appears after moving into a newer building, with the same cat that caused no trouble before.

Grooming does not remove the protein and nobody can honestly promise it will. What a proper session does is wash off a large share of the dried saliva and loose dander currently on the coat, and remove the dead undercoat that would otherwise shed continuously and carry that protein around the flat. Households that move to a four-week rhythm generally report a real, if partial, improvement. Combine it with an air purifier, keeping the bedroom cat-free, and washing bedding weekly, and it is usually enough. If it is not, that is a conversation for a doctor rather than a groomer — but do not rehome the cat before trying the boring version first.

Booking Around a Working-From-Home Day

Wakad runs on calls. We can work around them.

A large share of our Wakad clients are on video calls for most of the working day, and the honest question they ask is how much noise a grooming session actually makes.

Less than you would think, but not none. Most of the session is silent — the inspection, the brush-out, claws, the comb-through, the ear clean. The bath is quiet running water behind a closed bathroom door. There is one genuinely noisy stretch: the dryer, which runs anywhere from ten minutes for a shorthair to forty for a thick double coat, at roughly the volume of a domestic hairdryer. Clippers, if a haircut is involved, are a low buzz rather than a whine.

The practical arrangement most people land on is to tell the groomer at the start when the calls are, and let them sequence around it — the quiet work first, the dryer during your lunch break. It works better than it sounds, because the order of a grooming session is fairly flexible once claws are done. Book a slot that starts an hour before a gap in your calendar rather than trying to squeeze it into a fifteen-minute window, and shut the door of the room you work in so the cat does not choose it as a hiding place halfway through.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between a bath and a full grooming session?

A bath alone is just washing. A full grooming session includes bath, blow-dry, brushing, nail trim, ear cleaning, eye area wipe, and a coat condition check. We always do the full session — a bath without the rest is incomplete cat care.

Do you do deshedding treatments specifically for heavy shedders?

Yes, deshedding is part of the standard service. For breeds like British Shorthairs, Persians, and Maine Coons who shed heavily, we use a deshedding shampoo and conditioner in addition to thorough pre and post-bath brushing. It significantly reduces home shedding for several weeks.

My cat is on medication — does that affect grooming?

Most medications don't affect grooming. If your cat is on something for a skin condition or has any restrictions on products used, mention it when booking and we'll confirm compatibility. We defer to vet instructions on any specific product restrictions.

Someone in our house has become allergic to the cat. Will regular grooming help enough to keep her?

Often, partially — and it is worth trying before making any bigger decision. Bathing removes a good share of the dried saliva protein and loose dander sitting on the coat, and deshedding stops that material being distributed around a sealed flat for the next month. Households that shift to a four-week rhythm usually report a genuine improvement rather than a cure. Pair it with an air purifier, a cat-free bedroom and weekly washing of soft furnishings. If the reaction is severe or involves breathing difficulty, that is a doctor's call, not ours, and we will not pretend otherwise.

I take video calls all day. How disruptive is the session going to be?

Mostly not at all. The inspection, brushing, nail trim, comb-through and ear clean are silent, and the bath is running water behind a closed door. The one noisy part is the dryer — ten minutes for a shorthair, up to forty for a thick coat, at about the volume of a domestic hairdryer. Tell the groomer when your calls are as soon as they arrive and they will reorder the session so the loud stretch lands in a gap. It works well; the sequence is flexible once claws are out of the way.

We have a dog as well. Does living with a dog change anything for the cat's session?

Only in the logistics. Put the dog in another room with the door closed for the whole visit — not because anything will go wrong, but because a cat that is already dealing with a stranger and a dryer does not need an excited dog adding to it, and most dogs find the grooming kit extremely interesting. If you want both animals groomed, we can do that in the same visit; separate rooms, separate equipment, and we clean everything down in between. Cats generally go first, as they are the ones who cope least well with waiting.

What is your last slot? I only get back from Hinjewadi after seven.

The last workable start is around 7 PM so the coat is fully dry before the groomer leaves. We run to 9 PM, but a session beginning at 8 either finishes rushed or finishes with a damp cat, and we will do neither. Wakad evening slots fill early in the week because of exactly this commute — book several days ahead.

Our society logs service visitors on an app. What details do you provide?

Tell us at booking and we will send the groomer's name and number ahead of the slot so the entry can be pre-approved. Several of the large Wakad townships now require this, and a pre-logged visit means the groomer walks in rather than standing at the gate while your slot runs down.

The roads flood in monsoon here. Do sessions get cancelled?

Rarely cancelled, sometimes moved. If the Wakad–Kalewadi stretch or the Dange Chowk approach is genuinely waterlogged we call and reschedule rather than send a groomer through it with electrical equipment. Rescheduling costs you nothing. Arriving late and rushing your cat's session is the outcome we are avoiding.

My cat came from abroad with us. Does an imported cat need different care here?

The coat does need adjusting to. A cat that grew its coat in a temperate climate meets Pune humidity and sheds heavily and unpredictably for the first year, and longhairs mat faster here than their owners are used to. We would start on a tighter interval than you kept previously and relax it once the coat has settled into a local rhythm.

Can you groom a cat in a high-rise without the balcony being a risk?

That is exactly why we work in one closed room with the door shut for the whole session. The genuine danger in any home cat groom is a startled cat bolting, and in a Wakad tower the balcony is where they go — cats have no reliable sense of height and a railing is not the barrier people assume. Keep balcony doors shut and the netting up while we are there.
