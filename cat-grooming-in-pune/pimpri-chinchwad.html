A Year of Cat Grooming in PCMC, Season by Season

The same cat needs different things in March and in August.

PCMC gets a harder version of Pune's weather than the city centre does — hotter in April because of the concrete and the industrial belt, wetter underfoot in the monsoon, and dustier all year. Cats here do best on a grooming plan that shifts with the calendar rather than a fixed routine repeated twelve times.

February to April is the big shed. The winter undercoat comes off in one enormous wave and this is when deshedding earns its keep — expect to want a session every four to five weeks through this stretch regardless of coat length, and expect to be startled by the volume of fur that comes out. May and June are about heat management: lighter conditioning, no heavy oils, and a hygiene trim rather than a full clip for longhaired cats. This is the point where people ask about lion cuts, and it is usually not necessary.

July to September is the difficult quarter. Humidity keeps the undercoat damp, mats form quickly and quietly, and fungal skin issues are at their annual peak — a damp cat in a PCMC monsoon is a genuine risk, not a theoretical one. We shorten the interval for longhaired cats, extend the drying stage considerably, and use an antifungal formula where a cat has a history of it. October to January is the easiest stretch: the coat thickens for winter, matting slows, and an eight-week rhythm is fine for most cats — as long as the coat is actually being combed at home while it grows in.

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover all parts of Pimpri-Chinchwad?

Yes — Pimpri, Chinchwad, Akurdi, Bhosari, Nigdi, Moshi, Kasarwadi, Pimple Saudagar and surrounding areas. If you're in PCMC and aren't sure whether we cover your pincode, just message us.

What's the difference between cat grooming and dog grooming?

Cats require different shampoo formulations (dog shampoo can be toxic to cats), different handling techniques (low-restraint, reading feline body language), different drying approaches (lower heat and noise), and significantly more patience. A groomer trained only on dogs is not well-suited for cat sessions.

Can I book grooming for my cat and another pet on the same day?

Yes, we can do back-to-back sessions in the same home. Cats and dogs should ideally be in separate rooms during each other's sessions to reduce anxiety on both sides.

The water in our building is very hard. Does that affect the grooming?

It does, and it is common right across the PCMC belt. Hard water stops shampoo rinsing out cleanly, so residue stays in the undercoat and the fur feels rough and looks dull even straight after a wash — which is why bathing at home often does not give the result people expect. Our groomers work around it with a clarifying first wash, a conditioner that stays effective in mineral-heavy water, and a much longer rinse than most people do at home. If your supply is particularly hard, the groomer will say so and suggest what to change in your own routine.

My cat disappears into the loft the moment anyone rings the bell. What then?

Then we would rather you shut the loft before we arrive than spend forty minutes negotiating with a cat wedged behind the water tank. The single most useful thing you can do is close off lofts, storage spaces and any gap behind furniture about half an hour before the slot, while the cat is still relaxed and in the open. If she does get up there anyway, we wait rather than reach — dragging a frightened cat out of a hiding place undoes any chance of a calm session. If she stays put, we will reschedule the remainder without charging for the return visit.

What time slots do you run across PCMC?

Nine in the morning to nine at night, every day of the week. Across Pimpri, Chinchwad, Akurdi, Nigdi, Bhosari and Moshi the evening slots after 6 PM and the whole weekend are the ones that fill first, because so many households here have both adults on a full-time schedule. Weekday mornings are usually available at a day or two's notice. PCMC is a wide area for us to cover, so we give an arrival window rather than an exact minute, and the groomer messages you when they set off.

Does the industrial dust here settle in a cat's coat?

It does, and indoor cats are not exempt — particulate comes in through windows and settles on every surface the cat then lies on. A cat self-grooming in the PCMC belt is swallowing more of it than a cat in a leafier part of the city. It is a real argument for keeping to the shorter end of the recommended interval rather than stretching it.

We live in an older chawl-style building with a small bathroom. Is that enough space?

Yes. A cat session needs one room that can be closed, a tap and a plug point — not a large space. Compact flats across Pimpri and Chinchwad gaon are a normal part of our week. A smaller room is often better for a nervous cat than a large one, because there are fewer places to bolt to.

I work rotating shifts. Can I get an unusual slot?

Anywhere in our 9 AM to 9 PM window, yes, and shift work is a common reason people book here. What we cannot do is a 6 AM or an overnight visit. If your off-day falls midweek that works in your favour — midweek daytime slots across PCMC are the easiest to get at short notice.

Is a woman groomer available if our household would prefer one?

Yes — tell us at booking and we will schedule accordingly. It is a routine request across PCMC and we would far rather arrange it than have you uncomfortable during a home visit. The only honest caveat is availability: it may mean a different day or a wider arrival window than your first choice.

My cat is not microchipped and has no collar. Should I be worried during the session?

It is worth taking seriously, which is why we work in one closed room with the door shut throughout. The one genuine risk in a home session is a startled cat getting out of the flat, and an unchipped cat that gets out in a dense area like this is very hard to recover. Keep the main door shut and tell any household member not to open it while the groomer is working.
