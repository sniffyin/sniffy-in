Grooming frequency

Indoor Cats Still Need Regular Professional Grooming

Self-grooming only goes so far.

Cats are excellent self-groomers but there's a lot they simply can't handle alone — nail overgrowth, ear wax buildup, mats in hard-to-reach spots, and the ongoing shedding that fills your furniture. A professional session every 6–8 weeks handles all of this systematically and also gives us the chance to catch anything abnormal early — skin issues, lumps, ear problems — that you might miss at home.

Coat & skin care

The Lion Cut — When It Is Right, and When It Is Not

The most requested and most misunderstood thing we do.

A lion cut takes the body coat down to a few millimetres while leaving the head, the lower legs and a tuft at the end of the tail — hence the name. On NIBM Road it is probably the single most asked-about service, usually somewhere around April.

There are cases where it is clearly the right call. A cat whose coat has matted beyond de-matting gets far more relief from a clip than from an hour of pulling. An elderly or arthritic cat who genuinely cannot maintain its own back half benefits enormously and permanently. A very heavy-coated cat with recurring skin problems underneath is easier to treat and to monitor once the coat is off.

And there are cases where it is not. A cat's coat is insulation in both directions — it keeps heat out as much as in — so shaving a healthy cat "for the summer" does less for its comfort than people assume, and removes its protection against sunburn on the ears, nose and belly, which is a real problem for a cat that suns itself on a balcony. Regrowth takes three to six months and does not always come back with the same texture; on some cats the guard hairs return patchily. Occasionally the coat never fully recovers its original look.

So we will do it, and we will tell you honestly which of those two situations your cat is in. If the answer is that a good deshedding session and a shorter interval would solve the problem without a clipper, we will say that first.

Frequently asked questions

My cat has an ingrown or overgrown nail — can you handle that?

Nail trimming is part of every session. If a nail has curled or grown into the pad, we handle it carefully and will let you know if it looks like there's been any injury that a vet should check. We won't attempt anything that requires veterinary intervention.

Can two cats be groomed in one visit?

Yes, we can groom multiple cats in a single home visit. Back-to-back sessions are fine. Pricing is per cat. Just mention how many cats when booking so we can allocate enough time.

Should I feed my cat before the grooming session?

A light meal a couple of hours before is fine. A very full cat may feel uncomfortable being handled, especially when being positioned for belly work or nail trimming. Avoid feeding right before the session.

My cat goes outdoors — how does that affect grooming frequency?

Outdoor cats pick up significantly more dirt, debris, and parasites than indoor cats. We recommend grooming every 4–6 weeks for outdoor cats, plus tick checks at every session. Mention the outdoor access when booking so we can bring appropriate products.

How long does the coat take to grow back after a lion cut?

Three to six months for most cats, depending on age, breed and general health. A young Persian regrows quickly and fully; an older cat or one with an underlying skin or thyroid condition can take longer and sometimes regrows unevenly. The guard hairs — the longer outer coat — come back last, so there is an in-between stage of a few weeks where the coat looks soft and slightly woolly. That is normal. It is also the main reason we talk you through the decision properly rather than just taking the booking.

Can the session happen on our balcony or in the society's pet area instead of inside the flat?

Inside is almost always better, and we would gently push back on the alternatives. A balcony means an open edge and a cat that may bolt; a shared society pet area means the smells of every other animal that has used it, which is the exact stress a home session exists to avoid. If your concern is mess indoors, that is solvable — the bath happens in your bathroom and the drying on a mat we bring and take away, so there is nothing to clean up afterwards. If there is a genuine constraint at your end, talk to us and we will work something out, but a fully enclosed indoor space is what we ask for.

Our township needs a gate pass for service visitors. How do we arrange that?

Tell us at booking and we will send the groomer's name and number ahead of the slot so you can raise the entry on the app or with the gate. The NIBM townships are strict about pre-approval, and without it the groomer waits at the barrier — which comes out of your session time rather than ours.

Which parts of the NIBM Road belt do you reach for cat grooming?

The NIBM Road spine itself, Undri, Mohammed Wadi and the large gated townships on either side. Several run to multiple phases under a single name, so mention your phase or tower at booking — arriving at the right society and the wrong gate is the most common reason a session starts late here.

My cat has asthma. Is the dryer safe for her?

It needs care, and you should tell us at booking. We use a low-noise dryer on its lowest setting, keep the airflow off the face entirely, work in a well-ventilated room and stop at any sign of laboured breathing. For an asthmatic cat we would also skip any perfume or finishing spray. If your vet has advised against bathing altogether, follow the vet.

We are relocating abroad with our cat. What should be done beforehand?

A full groom two to three days before the flight — nails short, coat combed out, no mats — so nothing needs handling on travel day. Do not bathe a cat the morning of a flight; a damp undercoat in a cold hold is genuinely uncomfortable. Schedule tightly around the vet health certificate, not around the grooming.

I travel often. Can my house staff be present instead of me?

Yes, provided the cat is comfortable with them and you are reachable by phone. Someone the cat knows needs to be in the house. What we will not do is make a decision that changes your cat — clipping down a matted coat, for instance — on a staff member's authority. That call comes to you directly, even if the groomer waits on the line.
