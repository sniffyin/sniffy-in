Proper Cat Grooming Is More Than a Bath

What actually goes into a complete session.

A lot of cat owners assume cat grooming means a quick wash and dry. A proper session covers far more — nail trimming (front and back), ear cleaning, eye area wiping, sanitary trim for longhair cats, de-matting if needed, and a full coat condition assessment. Our groomers are trained to spot early signs of ear infections, skin irritation, and other issues during the session, which is something a rushed salon visit rarely catches.

Pune seasons

Winter in Kothrud Catches Cat Owners Out Every Year

The coat change nobody plans for.

Pune winters are mild by any sensible standard, and that is exactly why people ignore them. But Kothrud sits low and the December-to-February nights here drop further than the daytime temperature suggests, and cats respond to the change in daylight rather than to the thermometer. From roughly late October the undercoat thickens noticeably, and by January most longhaired cats in this area are carrying substantially more coat than they were in September.

Two things follow, and they arrive together. First, the thicker undercoat mats much more readily, because there is simply more of it packed close to the skin — a Persian that went eight weeks between sessions comfortably in the monsoon will be knotting at six in January. Second, and less obviously, owners bathe their cats less in winter because it feels unkind, so the interval stretches at exactly the point it should be shortening.

Our advice for Kothrud is unglamorous: keep the schedule the same or slightly tighter through winter, and let us handle the drying properly rather than skipping the bath. A cat dried completely on low heat is not cold — a cat left damp is, and that is the actual risk people are half-remembering. Then expect a heavy shed in March when the whole winter coat comes off at once, and book accordingly.

Coat & skin care

De-Matting: What We Can Save, and What Has to Come Off

The decision we explain before we make it.

De-matting is the part of the job with an actual judgement call in it, and it is worth knowing how that call gets made before your groomer is standing in your bathroom making it.

A mat that is loose enough to move against the skin can usually be saved. The groomer works detangler into it, holds the fur at the base so the pull is on their fingers rather than on your cat's skin, and teases it apart from the outer edge inward with a comb, a few strands at a time. It is slow and it is the reason a matted cat takes ninety minutes instead of sixty. Done properly the cat keeps its coat and feels nothing worse than mild tugging.

A mat that is tight and flat against the skin cannot be saved, and attempting it is the wrong answer. The skin underneath a set mat is often already inflamed, sometimes broken, and it lifts with the mat when you pull — which is how cats get cut during de-matting. In that case we clip it out at the base with a guarded blade, leaving a short patch that will regrow within a couple of months. We will tell you before we do it, show you the skin underneath, and explain why.

The honest summary: nearly every mat is preventable with a comb and five minutes a day, and nearly every mat we clip out belonged to a cat whose owner did not know the coat had knotted underneath. If we clip one, ask the groomer to show you where it formed. That is where the next one will be.

Frequently asked questions

My cat has mobility issues — can you still groom her at home?

Yes, and this is actually one of the strongest arguments for at-home grooming. We can position the cat on a soft surface, work with their mobility limitations, and avoid any postures that cause discomfort. Arthritic and disabled cats are much better served at home than at a salon.

Do you use a hair dryer on cats? She's terrified of the noise.

We use a low-noise dryer on the lowest heat setting, held at a distance. For cats who are very dryer-anxious, we can towel-dry most of the way and use the dryer minimally at the end. Let us know at booking and we'll plan around it.

Can you do a lion cut for my Persian?

Yes, lion cuts are available as an add-on to the standard grooming package. They're often recommended for heavily matted Persians or cats who struggle with coat maintenance between sessions. Mention it when booking so we allocate enough time.

How do I prepare my home before the groomer arrives?

Just a clear space in one room — bathroom, kitchen, or any area that can be wiped down easily. Some water access and a power outlet for the dryer. We bring the rest.

My cat is strictly indoor and never gets dirty. Does she really need professional grooming?

Being clean and being groomed are different things, and an indoor cat is genuinely cleaner than most. What she cannot do for herself is trim a claw that is starting to curve towards the pad, clear wax from the bend of her own ear canal, or remove the dead undercoat sitting close to her skin — which she instead swallows and brings back up on your floor. Indoor cats also tend to be less active, and a less active cat is more likely to be carrying weight that stops it reaching its own back. An eight to ten week interval is usually plenty for a healthy indoor shorthair. That is much less than a Persian needs, but it is not zero.

For a sound-sensitive cat the better plan is a session in the quiet week before the festival — a cat coping with a dryer and drumming outside is being asked for too much.

We live on the fourth floor of an old building with no lift. Is that workable?

Entirely. The groomer carries everything in one shoulder bag and the cat never leaves the flat, which is the whole point — no carrier, no stairs, no car. Older Kothrud walk-ups are among the most common addresses we work in.

Our water comes for two hours a day. Is that enough?

Easily. A cat bath uses far less water than people expect — two filled buckets are plenty for a full session including the rinse. Keep them ready before the slot and mention it to the groomer on arrival so they plan the rinse from stored water.

My cat scratches the furniture badly. Will nail trimming actually stop that?

It reduces the damage, not the behaviour. Trimming takes the sharp hook off the claw so the same scratching does much less to your sofa, but a cat scratches to mark and to stretch, and no trim removes that instinct. Nail trims plus a scratching post the cat actually likes is the combination that works. We do not apply claw caps and we would not recommend declawing under any circumstances.

Can I bathe my cat myself between your sessions?

You can, but for most cats you should not need to, and doing it too often strips the coat oils that keep the skin healthy. If you do, use a cat-specific shampoo only — dog shampoo is genuinely unsafe on cats — and the part people get wrong is drying. A cat left damp in Pune humidity stays wet at the skin for hours, which is how fungal patches begin.
