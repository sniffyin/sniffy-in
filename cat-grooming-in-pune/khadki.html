Old Bungalows, Big Compounds, and Cats Who Consider It All Theirs

Khadki cats patrol more territory than most.

Khadki still has what most of Pune has built over — walled compounds, old trees, service lanes, and quarters with actual gardens. Cats here are rarely confined to a flat. They have a route: the boundary wall, the shaded bit under the gulmohar, the back of the kitchen block, and whichever neighbour is softest about food.

That life shows up in the coat. Garden soil works deep into a dense undercoat and does not rinse out in one pass. Old compound walls mean scrapes and the occasional torn claw. Long grass in the monsoon months means ticks, and Khadki's tree cover keeps the ground damp enough that tick season here runs longer than in the concrete parts of the city. So our sessions in this area are built around a real inspection: between the toes, inside the ears, along the jawline and under the tail, before anything else happens. We would rather find a tick and tell you than produce a beautifully finished cat with a problem hidden under the fur.

Frequently asked questions

Can you groom a very old cat (14+ years)?

Yes, and it's important that you do. Senior cats need grooming support as their self-grooming ability decreases with age. We handle old cats with extra care — softer products, gentler handling, shorter segments with more rests, and particular attention to arthritic joints. Age 14+ is not too old for professional grooming.

What if the cat is partially paralysed or has limited mobility?

We adapt fully to physical limitations. A cat with limited mobility is positioned comfortably and we work around their range of movement. It may take longer but we complete a thorough session without causing additional stress.

How do I know the groomer is trained specifically for cats?

Our groomers complete cat-specific training including feline body language, low-restraint handling, cat-safe product knowledge, and breed-specific coat care. Ask us about our training protocols — we're transparent about what our groomers know.

Our address is inside the cantonment. Does the groomer need entry permission?

Sometimes, and it is worth sorting out before the slot rather than at the gate. Several Khadki addresses need the resident to inform security or leave a name at the entry point for a visitor. Tell us at booking and we will send the groomer's name, phone number and vehicle details in advance so you can pass them on. Without that, a groomer held at a gate eats into your slot and everyone's afternoon.

My cat keeps shaking her head and has dark wax in one ear. Will grooming fix it?

Grooming will clean the ear, but one-sided dark wax with head-shaking usually means ear mites or an infection, and that needs a vet rather than a groomer. We will clean gently and carefully, avoid flushing anything into an ear that may already be inflamed, and tell you what we can see. Booking a vet visit alongside the grooming is the right move — cleaning an ear without treating the cause just resets the clock.

We also feed a few community cats in the compound. Can they be groomed?

Honestly, usually not — and we would rather say so than take the booking. A cat that has never been handled is not going to tolerate a bath and a dryer from a stranger, and forcing that is bad for the cat and unsafe for the groomer. What sometimes works is a semi-tame compound cat who already lets you pick them up, in which case we would attempt claws and a coat check only, no water. If a community cat has a matted or injured coat, a vet with sedation facilities is the right route, not us.

Which areas around Khadki do you serve for cats?

Dapodi, Sangvi, Pimple Gurav, the cantonment area itself and across to the Aundh border. It is a compact, well-connected belt, so arrival windows here are among the most reliable we offer — which matters for cats, where a groomer arriving late to an already-alert animal makes the whole session harder.

Our building has no lift and we are on the third floor. Is that a problem?

Not at all — the groomer carries everything in a single shoulder bag, and your cat never leaves the flat. That is the whole advantage over a salon: no carrier, no stairs for the cat, no car journey. Older Khadki walk-ups are a routine address for us.

What happens to the fur and clippings after the session?

They leave with the groomer. Fur is collected as we go rather than swept at the end, nail clippings are bagged, and the working area is cleaned before the groomer leaves. You should not be finding traces of the session the next morning — if you do, tell us, because that falls short of the standard.

Can I book a nail trim on its own?

Yes. A short maintenance visit covering nails and an ear clean is available below full session price — ask for it by name at booking. For an indoor shorthair it is often all that is genuinely needed between full grooms, and it avoids bathing a cat more often than the coat requires.

Our water supply is on fixed timings. Is that enough for a cat?

Comfortably. A cat bath uses far less water than most people expect — two filled buckets cover wash and rinse for a full session. Keep them ready before the slot and tell the groomer on arrival so they plan the rinse from stored water rather than assuming a running tap.
