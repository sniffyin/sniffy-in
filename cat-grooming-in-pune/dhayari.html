Row Houses, Terraces, and Cats Who Live Half Outdoors

Dhayari cats get more outside time than most Pune cats.

A lot of Dhayari is plotted development — row houses, independent homes, low-rise buildings with accessible terraces. That gives cats here something most Pune apartment cats do not have: a genuine half-outdoor life. They sit on parapets, walk the compound wall, and come back in with whatever they picked up out there.

It changes the grooming brief completely. A strictly indoor cat in a Kothrud tower mostly needs coat maintenance. A Dhayari terrace cat needs that plus a real inspection — ticks behind the ears and between the toes, grass seeds and burrs in the belly fur, small scratches hidden under the coat, and paw pads that have been walking on hot concrete all summer. We build that check into every session here rather than treating it as an add-on, and we will tell you plainly if something needs a vet rather than a groomer.

Coat & skin care

Why Brushing at Home Never Quite Fixes the Shedding

The fur you can see is not the problem.

Almost every Dhayari cat parent we meet already brushes their cat. And they are usually surprised by how much fur comes off during a professional deshedding pass anyway. The reason is simple: a normal brush lifts loose fur off the top of the coat. It does not reach the dead undercoat sitting close to the skin, which is where the bulk of it is and where it stays until it either falls out around your house or ends up swallowed during self-grooming.

That second route is the one worth caring about. A cat that is ingesting a season's worth of loose undercoat is a cat bringing up hairballs on your floor and, occasionally, a cat with a real digestive problem. A proper deshedding session with the right combs removes that layer in one go. Most Dhayari clients tell us the difference around the house is obvious for about three weeks, which is a reasonable guide to how often it is worth repeating.

Frequently asked questions

Can you start grooming a kitten? How young is too young?

We can groom kittens from about 12 weeks onwards for basic sessions — gentle bath, nail check, ear inspection. Full grooming sessions with blow-drying are better started at 16 weeks or so when they're a bit more physically robust. Early grooming habituation is excellent for coat maintenance later in life.

What if my cat gets sick during the session?

It's rare but possible, especially with very anxious cats. If a cat shows signs of significant distress (excessive panting, trying to hide, repeated vomiting) we stop the session, let the cat settle, and complete what we can. Safety always comes first.

Do you use any calming sprays or supplements for anxious cats?

We don't administer any supplements or medications. For extremely anxious cats, some owners choose to use a vet-approved calming supplement at home before the session — that's a conversation for you and your vet. What we do is use calm body language, slow movements, and low-restraint technique which is the most effective approach for the majority of cats.

Do you cover Narhe, Nanded City and the Sinhagad Road side of Dhayari?

Yes. Dhayari Phata, Narhe, Nanded City, Kirkatwadi and the stretch along Sinhagad Road are all within our regular route, along with Ambegaon and Warje on the other side. If your society is somewhere between two of those and you are not sure, send us the location pin and we will confirm before you book.

My cat sheds constantly. How many sessions before I actually notice less fur at home?

Usually one. A proper deshedding pass removes the dead undercoat that ordinary brushing leaves behind, and most owners see a clear drop in fur on furniture within a day or two. The effect fades over roughly three weeks as new coat loosens, which is why heavy shedders in Dhayari tend to settle into a four to six week rhythm rather than the eight weeks a low-shedding cat needs.

We live on the fourth floor of a building with no lift. Is that a problem?

Not at all — walk-ups are common in Dhayari and our kit is designed to be carried. The groomer brings everything up in one trip: clippers, dryer, combs, shampoos, towels and a mat. Nothing heavy, nothing that needs to come back down mid-session. Just let us know at booking so the groomer can plan a few extra minutes.

We are on borewell water. Does hard water affect a cat's coat?

It does, and it is a real issue across the Dhayari and Narhe side. Hard water leaves mineral residue that dulls the coat and can leave skin itchy even after what felt like a proper rinse. Our groomers rinse for longer where the water is hard and finish with a conditioning rinse to counter it — if home baths have left your cat's coat looking flat, this is usually the reason.

How far out from Dhayari do you travel for a cat session?

Out to Kirkatwadi, Nanded City, Narhe and the plotted developments toward Sinhagad Road. Lane names repeat a great deal in this belt, so send a location pin with the booking — a groomer circling to find your gate is time taken out of the cat’s settling period, not ours.

Our cat comes and goes from the compound. What should we watch for?

Ticks first, and they are why we inspect before we wash rather than after. A cat with free compound access in this belt picks up considerably more than an indoor cat, and the places to check are between the toes, inside both ears, along the jawline and at the tail base. If we find something, we show you where it was rather than treating it silently.

Power cuts are frequent here. What happens if the electricity goes?

Only the drying stage needs power, so a cut pauses the session rather than ending it. Tell the groomer which sockets run off your inverter if you have one. We will not leave a cat damp and go — if power is genuinely out we towel-dry thoroughly, keep the room warm and wait for it to come back.

We took in a street cat. Does she need the same grooming as a pedigree?

Less bathing, the same inspections — arguably more of them. Street-origin cats generally have practical, self-maintaining shorthair coats and over-bathing does them no favours at all. What earns its keep every visit is the tick and flea check, nail trimming, and a proper look at the ears and skin, since these are the cats most likely to have picked something up.
