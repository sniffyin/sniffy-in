Coat & skin care

Diet, Coat Quality, and the Limits of What Grooming Can Do

A dull coat is sometimes a grooming problem. Often it is not.

We get asked this regularly in Kharadi, usually by people who are doing everything right and cannot understand why the coat still looks flat: the cat is groomed on schedule, brushed at home, and the fur is still dry, dull or shedding more than it should.

The honest answer is that a coat is an output, not an input. Roughly a quarter of the protein a cat eats goes into growing hair and maintaining skin, which is an enormous share, and it is the first thing the body deprioritises when something is off. So a persistently poor coat despite good grooming is usually pointing at one of four things: a diet too low in animal protein or essential fatty acids, which is common with cheap dry-only feeding; insufficient water intake, which is very common in cats fed exclusively dry food in an air-conditioned flat; a parasite load; or an underlying condition such as thyroid or kidney disease, particularly in an older cat.

What grooming genuinely does is remove dead coat, distribute skin oils along the hair shaft, clear buildup that dulls the surface, and catch problems early. What it cannot do is build a good coat out of poor raw material. If your groomer keeps flagging the same dryness session after session and nothing changes, the next conversation is with a vet about diet and bloodwork, not with us about a different shampoo. We would rather tell you that than sell you a conditioning add-on that will not fix it.

What to Ask Before You Book Any Cat Groomer

Including us. Especially us.

Kharadi is full of people who research a purchase properly, so here is the list we would want someone to run us through. It is genuinely useful whether or not you end up booking with Sniffy.

Ask whether the groomer is trained specifically on cats, not just willing to do them. Most grooming in India is dog grooming with cats accepted reluctantly, and the handling, the products and the pacing are all different. Ask what shampoo goes on the cat and whether it is cat-formulated — dog shampoo is the single most common thing that goes wrong, because several ingredients that are routine for dogs are not safe on feline skin. Ask whether they scruff, bag or cage-dry; if the answer is yes to any of them, understand that you are being told the cat will be immobilised rather than settled.

Then the boring operational questions, which matter more than people think. Is the price published before the session or quoted at the end? Is equipment cleaned between animals? What happens if the cat will not tolerate the session — do you get charged in full? Will the same groomer come back next time?

You are welcome to ask us all of it before booking, and we will answer plainly. If any of the answers ever stops matching what happens in your flat, tell us — that gap is the only thing that actually damages a service like this.

Frequently asked questions

Can I schedule recurring monthly bookings?

Yes, recurring bookings are available and recommended for longhair breeds. You can set up a standing monthly or bi-monthly slot and you'll always get the same groomer when possible, which helps cats who take time to warm up to new people.

My cat has never been bathed — how do I prepare for the first session?

Not much preparation is needed from your side. Just have a clear space available in one room and make sure the cat's nails haven't been clipped recently (we'll do that). Let the groomer know it's the first bath — we start extra slowly with first-timers and set realistic expectations.

Do you use cage dryers or conventional dryers?

We never use cage dryers. We use hand-held dryers on low heat, held at a safe distance from the cat. For cats who are very dryer-anxious, towel drying is the primary method and the hand dryer is used minimally at the end just to ensure the coat is properly dry.

How do I tell whether a dull coat is a grooming problem or a health problem?

Timing is the clearest signal. A coat that looks good for two or three weeks after a session and then gradually dulls is a grooming-interval problem — book a little more often. A coat that still looks flat and dry a few days after a proper wash and deshedding is telling you something else, usually diet, hydration, parasites or an underlying condition, and it needs a vet rather than a different shampoo. Sudden changes matter too: a coat that becomes greasy, clumped or patchy over a few weeks in a cat whose routine has not changed is worth investigating quickly, particularly in an older cat.

Can I get an invoice or receipt for the session?

Yes — just ask the groomer at the end of the session or message us afterwards, and we will send one across on WhatsApp with the package, any add-ons and the total. Payment itself is taken at the end of the session by cash or UPI, once you have seen the work. Several of our Kharadi clients need a paper trail for expense or society records; it is a routine request and not a problem.

Yes. Kharadi proper, the EON and World Trade Centre side, Pallavi Nagar, the Nagar Road stretch, Wagholi, Mundhwa and Kalyani Nagar are all on the same eastern route. The bypass floods in heavy rain. Do sessions still go ahead?

Usually yes, occasionally moved. If the Kharadi bypass or the approach to your society is genuinely waterlogged we call and reschedule rather than send a groomer through it with electrical equipment. There is no charge for that. A soaked groomer with a wet dryer bag is no use to your cat.

Our tower has a pet policy and a separate service entrance. What do you need from us?

Just tell us at booking and pass the groomer details to your society office beforehand. Most large Kharadi towers now log service visitors on an app, and a pre-approved entry means the groomer comes straight up rather than waiting at the desk while your slot runs down.

We have a dog too. Where should the dog be during the cat's session?

In a different room with the door shut, for the whole session. Even a dog the cat lives with happily changes how a cat behaves under handling, and a dog that comes in mid-bath will undo twenty minutes of settling. If both need grooming, we do the cat first while the house is quiet and before there is dog fur and dryer noise in the air.

My cat is on a prescription diet and very food-motivated. Can treats be used during the session?

Only yours, and only if you hand them over. We do not bring treats — a cat on a prescription diet, a food allergy or a weight plan can be set back by something well-intentioned from a stranger. If you want treats used as a settler, leave a few of your own out and tell the groomer they are approved.

Do you cover the EON side and the newer towers toward Wagholi?

Yes, the whole Kharadi belt including the EON Free Zone side and out toward the Wagholi border. Several complexes here share a builder name across phases, so mention your phase or tower at booking — it is the usual reason a groomer arrives at the right society and the wrong entrance.
