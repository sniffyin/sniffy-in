Hairballs Are a Grooming Problem Wearing a Digestive Costume

Where the fur on your floor actually comes from.

A cat spends a substantial part of its waking life grooming itself, and a cat's tongue is covered in backward-facing barbs that work like a comb. Everything those barbs collect goes one way: down. In a cat with a normal coat and a normal amount of loose fur, that swallowed hair passes through and you never know about it.

The problem starts when the volume goes up. A heavy-shedding cat, a longhaired cat in a Baner high-rise with the AC running, or a cat approaching a seasonal moult is ingesting far more hair than its gut can move along, and the surplus collects in the stomach. That is a hairball, and the retching-then-producing routine on your floor at six in the morning is the successful outcome. The unsuccessful outcome is the hair staying put, which causes loss of appetite, constipation and occasionally a genuine blockage.

The point worth taking from this is that hairball frequency is a direct readout of how much loose fur is in your cat's coat, and that is something a grooming schedule controls. Removing the dead undercoat with a proper deshedding pass means it ends up in a bag rather than in your cat. Owners in Baner who move a heavy shedder from a two-month to a four-week rhythm generally report the hairballs dropping off noticeably. What is not normal, and no amount of grooming will fix, is repeated retching that produces nothing, or a cat that goes off food alongside it. That is a vet visit the same week.

Services explained

The Same Groomer, As Often As We Can Manage It

Cats do not warm to a service. They warm to a person.

Baner runs on efficiency and most of our clients here would happily accept whichever groomer is free. We try not to give you that, and it is worth explaining why we bother.

A dog will generally cooperate with a competent stranger. A cat will not — it cooperates with someone it has decided is acceptable, and that decision is made once and then largely stands. A groomer who has been to your flat twice already knows that your cat has to sniff the bag before anything starts, that she will tolerate her back feet but not her front ones, that the dryer has to come from the left, and that there is a mat that forms behind her right elbow every single time. None of that is written down usefully; it is knowledge that lives with a person. The second session with the same groomer is noticeably faster than the first, and the fourth barely resembles the first at all.

So we route repeat bookings back to the same groomer wherever the schedule allows, and if you ask for someone by name we will do our best to make it work — which occasionally means a slightly later slot than you wanted. When it genuinely cannot happen, the new groomer arrives having read the notes from the previous sessions, so at least the cat is not starting from nothing. If a particular groomer worked especially well with your cat, tell us. We would rather build the schedule around that than around convenience.

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover Balewadi and Sus Road areas near Baner?

Yes — Balewadi, Sus Road, Baner Main Road, Pashan, and surrounding areas are all within the coverage zone.

Can the groomer handle a very large cat like a Maine Coon?

Yes. Maine Coons and other large breeds are something our groomers handle regularly. The session takes longer than a standard cat and we price and schedule accordingly. Let us know the breed when booking.

What's the minimum age for a first grooming session?

For a gentle introductory session, 12 weeks is fine. For a full grooming session with bath and dryer, we prefer 16 weeks — kittens before this are still temperature-regulating and a full wash carries more risk. Nail checks and ear checks can happen earlier.

Can the same groomer handle every visit for our cat?

Yes, and we encourage it. Cats accept a specific person rather than a service, and the difference between a first and a third session with the same groomer is substantial — they already know your cat's particular objections and where her coat knots. Mention the name when you book and we will route it that way wherever the schedule allows, which occasionally means offering you a slot an hour or two off your first choice. If it genuinely cannot be arranged, the groomer who does come will have read the notes from previous visits.

The fur at the base of my cat's tail has gone greasy and clumped, but the rest of the coat is fine. What is that?

That is almost certainly stud tail — an overactive gland on the upper surface of the tail base producing excess oil. It is most common in unneutered males but neutered cats and females get it too, and it is harmless in itself, just unpleasant. It responds well to a degreasing wash concentrated on that area during the session, and to a shorter grooming interval so the buildup never gets established. If the skin there is red, sore or has black crusting rather than just greasy fur, that has probably become infected and needs a vet.

My cat was spayed ten days ago. Is it safe to book a grooming session?

Give it a bit longer — around fourteen days after a routine spay, and only once your vet is happy the incision has fully closed with no scabbing or weeping. Water on a healing surgical site is the specific thing to avoid. Once it has healed we will still work gently around the area and keep shampoo off it, and the shaved patch will regrow over a couple of months. If she needs claws or an ear clean before then, we can do a short water-free session instead — just tell us the surgery date when you book.

What weekend availability do you have? Weekdays are impossible.

Saturday and Sunday run the same 9 AM to 9 PM hours, and Baner weekend slots are the fastest-filling we have — the IT working week concentrates demand into two days here more than anywhere else. Book four or five days ahead rather than trying on the Friday.

Construction dust from the Sus and Balewadi side is constant. Does that reach an indoor cat?

Yes. Fine construction dust drifts indoors and settles on every surface the cat sleeps on, and from there it goes into the coat and gets swallowed during self-grooming. While building work continues nearby, keeping to the shorter end of your cat's interval is a reasonable response.

I will be on video calls throughout. How disruptive is a cat session?

Less than you would expect, with one exception. Most of the session is quiet handling in a closed room; the dryer is the only genuinely audible stage and it runs for perhaps fifteen minutes. Tell the groomer if you have a fixed call and they will sequence the noisy part around it where the coat allows.

Our cat is strictly indoor in a high-rise. Is the balcony a risk during the session?

It is the main one, which is why we work in a single closed room with the door shut throughout. A startled cat in a Baner tower heads for the balcony, and cats have no reliable sense of height — a railing is not the barrier people assume it is. Keep balcony doors closed and any netting up while we are working.

Can you groom a cat that has just moved house with us?

Give it two to three weeks first, unless the coat is genuinely matted and cannot wait. A cat that has just relocated is already spending its whole reserve on the new environment, and adding a stranger with a dryer on top of that usually produces a bad first session that makes the next one harder. If matting means it cannot wait, tell us and we will keep the session minimal.
