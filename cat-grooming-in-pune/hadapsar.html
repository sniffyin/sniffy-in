Cat grooming

What "Cat-Safe Products" Actually Means

It is a real distinction, not a marketing line.

Every grooming service in Pune will tell you it uses cat-safe products. Very few will tell you what makes something unsafe, so here is the substance behind the phrase.

Cats metabolise differently from dogs and from us. They lack much of the liver enzyme activity that breaks down certain plant compounds, so substances that a dog processes without difficulty accumulate in a cat. Tea tree oil is the well-known example and is genuinely dangerous to cats even in small amounts, but the same caution applies to most essential oils used as fragrance — eucalyptus, citrus, pine, lavender. Permethrin, an insecticide found in many dog flea products, is severely toxic to cats and is one of the more common causes of accidental poisoning we hear about; a dog spot-on applied to a cat is a veterinary emergency.

Then there is pH, which is less dramatic but matters every session. Cat skin sits at a different pH from dog skin and is markedly thinner. A dog shampoo will strip a cat's skin oils, and the cat then over-grooms the dryness, which is how a bath produces a scratching cat a week later.

The last point is the one people forget: a cat licks its entire coat clean within hours of a bath. Whatever residue is left on the fur is going to be eaten. That is why the rinse matters as much as the shampoo, and why we will not use anything on a cat that we would not be comfortable with it swallowing. If you want to know exactly what is going on your cat in Hadapsar, ask the groomer to show you the bottle. Any groomer who hesitates at that question has told you something useful.

Teeth, and the Things Grooming Does Not Cover

A short list of what to take somewhere else.

Dental disease is the most common health problem in adult cats and one of the most under-treated, and it comes up in Hadapsar conversations often enough to be worth addressing directly: no, we do not clean teeth, and you should be wary of any groomer who offers to.

Scaling a cat's teeth properly requires general anaesthesia, because the disease that matters is below the gum line where no conscious cat will permit anyone to work. So-called anaesthesia-free dental cleaning scrapes visible tartar off the crown, which makes the tooth look better and does nothing whatsoever about the periodontal disease underneath — it can actively delay the vet visit that would have caught it. It is a cosmetic procedure sold as a medical one.

What we will do is look and tell you. A groomer has a cat's head in their hands for an hour and will notice a strong smell from the mouth, visible tartar, a red gum line, or a cat that flinches when the jaw area is touched. Any of those means a vet.

The same boundary applies elsewhere. We do not express anal glands — that is a veterinary procedure and doing it badly causes real damage. We do not administer medication or sedatives of any kind. We do not diagnose skin conditions or supply treatment for them. And we will not attempt a groom on a cat that seems genuinely unwell rather than merely unwilling. Being clear about the edges of the job is part of doing it properly.

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover Manjri and Phursungi areas near Hadapsar?

Yes — Hadapsar main area, Magarpatta, Keshavnagar, Manjri, and Phursungi are all within coverage. Message us with your pincode if you're on the outer edge.

My cat came from a breeding environment — how do I handle their first grooming session?

Cats from breeding environments often haven't had individualised, calm grooming experiences. They may be used to being handled but not necessarily in a gentle one-on-one manner. We approach these cats with extra patience during the introduction phase and build trust before beginning. First sessions from breeding backgrounds typically go better than owners expect.

Can you tell if my cat needs a vet visit based on what you see during grooming?

We can flag observations — ear redness, skin conditions, unusual lumps, dental concerns, or abnormal nail growth — but we're groomers, not vets. We'll tell you what we noticed and recommend a vet follow-up for anything that warrants it. We'd rather flag something unnecessary than miss something important.

Do you cover Magarpatta, Amanora, Keshavnagar and the Manjri side?

Yes — Hadapsar is one of our larger coverage areas and it includes Magarpatta City, Amanora, Keshavnagar, Manjri, Phursungi, Mundhwa and the Solapur Road stretch. The gated townships need the groomer registered at the gate, so tell us at booking and we will send their name and number in advance for you to pass to security. It saves the slot being eaten by a wait at the entrance.

Can you brush my cat's teeth as part of the session?

No, and we would gently steer you away from anyone who says yes. Meaningful dental work on a cat happens below the gum line and requires anaesthesia — scraping visible tartar off a conscious cat improves the look of the tooth and does nothing about the disease underneath, which is the part that matters. What we will do is notice: a strong smell from the mouth, a red gum line, visible tartar, or flinching when the jaw is handled. If we see any of that we will tell you, and it is worth a vet appointment rather than a grooming add-on.

My cat licks herself thoroughly right after a bath. Is the shampoo safe if she swallows some?

It has to be, and that is exactly why product choice matters more for cats than for dogs. Everything left on the coat gets ingested within a few hours, so we use cat-formulated, pH-balanced shampoo with no essential oils, and we rinse for considerably longer than the coat looks like it needs — under-rinsing is the actual risk, not the shampoo itself. We never use dog products on cats; several ingredients that are routine for dogs, permethrin in particular, are seriously toxic to cats. If you want to see what is being used, ask the groomer to show you the bottle.

Magarpatta and Amanora need a gate pass. How do we arrange it?

Tell us at booking and we will send the groomer's name and number ahead of the slot so you can raise the entry on the township app or with the gate. Both are strict about pre-approval, and without it the groomer waits at the barrier — which comes out of your session time rather than ours.

Is an evening session harder on a cat than a daytime one?

Slightly, and the last workable start is around 7 PM so the coat is fully dry before the groomer leaves. Cats are naturally more active and more reactive around dusk, so a mid-morning slot is genuinely calmer if your schedule allows it. If evening is the only option, book several days ahead — those slots go early across Hadapsar and Magarpatta.

We have a dog as well. Where should the dog be?

In a different room with the door shut for the whole session. Even a dog your cat lives with happily changes how the cat behaves under handling, and a dog wandering in mid-bath undoes twenty minutes of settling. If both need grooming, we do the cat first, while the house is quiet and before there is dog fur and dryer noise around.

Can you groom a cat that has just had a litter?

A light session, yes, once the kittens are a few weeks old — nails, a gentle comb-out, and any soiling around the hind end cleaned. A full bath while she is nursing is something we would avoid: it strips coat scent the kittens navigate by and adds stress at the point she has least to spare. Tell us at booking and we will keep it minimal.

Do the township towers here cause any delay getting in?

Only when entry has not been pre-approved. The groomer uses whichever lift your building requires and carries everything in one bag, so that part is trivial — the delay comes from standing at a gate. Flag it at booking if your service entrance sits on a different side of the complex from the main gate.
